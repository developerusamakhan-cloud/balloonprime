## Near-original quality under 2MB

A 2MB limit is common on upload forms, print and photo-book services, support desks, real-estate and car listing sites, and some government portals that accept high-quality photos. Modern phones and cameras often produce files of 4–15MB, which is too large. At 2MB you can keep almost all of the detail, so the result looks like the original.

This page is set to a 2MB target with no width limit. The compressor keeps the full resolution wherever possible and lowers the quality only slightly.

## How to compress to 2MB

1. **Drop your photos** or click **Choose files**. Up to 20 at a time.
2. **Wait for the Fits target badge** on each image.
3. **Download** one by one or with **Download all**.

Use the quick buttons for 500KB, 1MB, 1.5MB or 2MB, or type your own number and unit.

## What to expect

| Original | Original size | Typical result under 2MB |
|---|---|---|
| Phone photo, 12MP | 3–5MB | Full size, very high quality |
| Phone photo, 48MP | 8–15MB | Full or slightly reduced size |
| Camera JPG, 24MP | 8–12MB | Full size, high quality |
| Large PNG screenshot | 3–6MB | Full size as JPG |

For most photos, the difference is invisible even when zoomed in on a computer screen.

## Good uses for 2MB

- **Photo printing and photo books**, where services cap uploads but you want maximum detail.
- **Property and vehicle listings**, where buyers zoom into photos.
- **Support and insurance claims**, where details in a photo matter.
- **Portfolio and competition entries** with a file size limit.
- **Archiving copies** that take far less space than the originals.

## Printing from a 2MB file

A 12-megapixel photo is about 4000×3000 pixels. Compressed to 2MB, it keeps that resolution, which prints sharply up to about 13×10 inches at 300 DPI. If you also need an exact print size, use the [resize in cm & inches](tool:resize-image-in-cm) tool first, then compress here. The compressor keeps the DPI value of JPG files, so the print size stays correct.

## When to choose a smaller target

If the image is only for screens, 2MB is more than you need. For websites, 200–500KB per image loads much faster. For email, 1MB per photo is usually plenty. See [compress to 1MB](tool:compress-image-to-1mb) and [compress to 500KB](tool:compress-image-to-500kb).

## Very large photos

Phones with 48- or 50-megapixel cameras create huge files. If even a lower quality cannot reach 2MB, the compressor reduces the dimensions in small steps. You can also set a **Maximum width** under **Advanced options**, for example 4000 pixels, which is still plenty for prints and screens.

## Tips for the best result

- **Start from the original file**, not a copy that was shared through a messaging app.
- **Avoid saving photos as PNG**; JPG is far more efficient for photographs.
- **Crop away what you do not need**, especially in panoramas.
- **Keep the originals** in a safe folder; compression is permanent.

For more on balancing size and quality, read [how to lower image file size without losing quality](post:how-to-lower-image-file-size), and for exact pixel sizes see [how to resize a picture in pixels](post:resize-picture-in-pixels).

## Private and unlimited

Your photos are processed by your own browser and never uploaded. There is no account, watermark or daily limit, so you can process a whole album in batches of 20.

## Works on all devices

Use it in Chrome, Safari, Edge or Firefox on Windows, Mac, Android and iPhone. iPhone HEIC photos are converted to JPG automatically, and results are saved to your Downloads folder or the Files app.

## 2MB vs the original

| | Original phone photo | 2MB copy |
|---|---|---|
| Resolution | 4032 × 3024 | Usually the same |
| File size | 3–5MB | Under 2MB |
| Visible difference on screen | – | None in most photos |
| Print up to A4 | Yes | Yes |

For most people, the 2MB copy is indistinguishable from the original while taking less than half the space. Keep the original anyway, for the rare case where you need every bit of detail.

## Before uploading to a print service

Check the service's maximum file size and recommended resolution, compress to that limit, and keep the original in case the service asks for a larger file. For photo books, process every photo with the same target so the whole book has consistent quality.
