## Tips for the cleanest result

- **Contrast helps.** A subject that stands out from the background gives the AI clear edges.
- **Keep the subject in focus.** Blurry edges are harder to separate.
- **Even lighting.** Strong shadows on the subject can be mistaken for background.
- **Fill the frame.** If the subject is small, crop closer first.
- **Start from the original photo**, not a screenshot.

## White vs transparent: which download?

Choose **white and JPG** when the photo goes straight into a form, a listing or a profile, because JPG files are smaller and accepted everywhere. Choose **transparent and PNG** when you plan to place the subject on a design, a slide or a poster, so you can pick the background later. You can switch between the two in the same session without processing the photo again.

## Getting a natural look

A cut-out on pure white can look flat if the original light was very warm or very dark. Take the photo in soft daylight, avoid strong coloured lamps, and keep the camera at the subject's eye level. If edges around hair look rough, try light grey instead of pure white; it hides small imperfections and still reads as a plain background. For product photos, keep the camera height and distance the same for every item so the whole catalogue looks consistent.
