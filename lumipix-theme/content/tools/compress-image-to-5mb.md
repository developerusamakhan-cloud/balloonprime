Use this tool to compress image to 5MB online, keeping full visible quality while getting large photos and scans under the limit.

## When you need an image under 5MB

A 5MB limit is common on job portals, school and university applications, support forms, marketplaces and some email services. Photos from modern cameras and phones, as well as high-resolution scans, can easily be 8MB, 12MB or more. They need to shrink, but at 5MB there is plenty of room to keep them looking exactly the same.

The compressor runs in your browser. Your files are processed on your own device and never uploaded.

## How to compress an image to 5MB

1. **Drop your images** or tap **Choose files**. You can add up to 20 at once.
2. **Check the target.** 5MB is already selected. Tap a quick size, such as 2MB or 3MB, if your form allows less.
3. **Download** each result or the whole batch as a ZIP. Every file shows its new size next to the original.

The compressor searches for the highest quality that still fits under the target, so you get the sharpest possible file instead of an over-compressed one.

## Will I lose quality?

At 5MB, almost never in a way you can see. A 12-megapixel photo saved as a high quality JPG is usually around 3MB to 6MB, so many photos only need light compression. Very large images, such as panoramas or 50-megapixel camera files, may also be scaled down slightly so they fit. Even then they keep far more pixels than any screen or standard print needs.

If you plan to print the photo large, keep the original as well and upload the compressed copy only where the limit applies.

## PNG, WebP and screenshots

PNG files are lossless and can be very large. The compressor saves its results as JPG, which usually brings a PNG photo or screenshot well under 5MB at once. Transparent areas become white in a JPG, so for a logo that must stay transparent, keep the PNG and make it smaller with the [image resizer](tool:image-resizer) instead. To convert many files without a size target, [PNG to JPG](tool:png-to-jpg) is another quick option.

## Scans and documents

Scanned pages at 600 DPI can be far larger than 5MB. Compressing them to 5MB keeps text crisp. If a form wants one file with several pages, compress the scans first and then combine them with [image to PDF](tool:image-to-pdf).
