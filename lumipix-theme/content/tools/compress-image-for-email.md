## Why photos get stuck in email

A single photo from a modern phone is often 3–5MB, and a camera photo can be over 10MB. Attach a handful from a holiday, a site visit or an event and the message can grow far beyond what an email service will accept. The result is familiar: a slow upload, a "message too large" warning, or an email that bounces back hours later.

Every email provider sets its own limit on the total size of attachments, and the limit often applies to the whole message, not to each file. Limits vary between providers and can change, so check the one you and your recipient use. Remember that the recipient's limit matters too: a message your service sends happily can still be refused at the other end.

Even when large attachments do get through, they eat into storage quotas, take ages to open on mobile data and are far bigger than anyone needs to view them on a screen.

## What this page does

This page opens with a **500KB** target per image and a maximum width of 2000 pixels. That keeps photos sharp on laptops and phones while cutting a typical phone picture to a fraction of its size. The quick buttons switch between **200KB**, **500KB**, **1MB** and **2MB**, and **Custom size** accepts any other value in KB or MB.

The compressor searches for the highest quality that fits each file under the target, and only reduces dimensions in small steps if it has to. A result badge confirms every image fits.

## How to compress photos for email

1. **Select your photos.** Drop up to 20 images onto the page or click **Choose files**. On a phone, pick them from your gallery.
2. **Choose a target.** Leave **500KB** for most emails, or pick a smaller one for long sets.
3. **Keep JPG selected** under **Save as**. Every email app and device opens JPG.
4. **Wait for the badges** confirming each file fits.
5. **Press Download all** to save one ZIP with every compressed photo.
6. **Attach and send.** Attach the ZIP, or unzip it and attach the photos individually so they preview in the message.

## Picking a size per photo

Work backwards from the attachment limit you need to stay under. Divide it by the number of photos, leave some headroom, and choose the nearest preset.

| Number of photos | Suggested target per photo | Approximate total |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | **2MB** | Up to about 6MB |
| 4–10 | **1MB** | Up to about 10MB |
| 10–20 | **500KB** | Up to about 10MB |
| 20 or more | **200KB** | About 4MB per 20 photos |

Attachments are encoded for sending, which makes them roughly a third larger in transit than on your device. That is why leaving headroom below the provider's limit is a good habit.

## Tips for sending photos by email

- **Compress before you attach**, not after the email fails. It saves time and repeated uploads.
- **Name your files clearly.** Each compressed copy keeps its name with "-compressed" added, so originals and copies are easy to tell apart.
- **Split very large sets** into two or three emails rather than one huge message.
- **Use JPG for photos.** It opens everywhere, while some older email apps do not preview WebP.
- **Keep the originals** in a safe folder. Compression is permanent, so always work on copies.
- **Turn documents into a PDF** if the recipient wants one file. [Image to PDF](tool:image-to-pdf) can combine compressed pages.

## Common problems and fixes

- **The email still says the message is too large.** Lower the target to **200KB**, send fewer photos per email, or check you attached the compressed copies, not the originals.
- **Photos look small when the recipient opens them.** Raise the target to **1MB** and **Maximum width (px, optional)** under **Advanced options** to around 3000.
- **The recipient cannot open the ZIP.** Most computers and phones open ZIP files directly, but attaching the photos individually avoids the question entirely.
- **iPhone photos will not open on Windows.** HEIC photos are converted to JPG automatically, so attach the downloaded copies.
- **The phone picks the original photo.** Attach from the Downloads folder on Android or the Files app on iPhone, where the compressed copies are saved.
- **Only one image needs to be smaller.** Use the main [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image) and type any limit.

## Email or a sharing link?

Email works best for a modest number of photos meant for viewing on screens. Compressed to 500KB, twenty holiday photos fit comfortably in a typical message. For full-resolution originals, printing or hundreds of pictures, a cloud sharing link is more practical, because it is not tied to attachment limits.

## Batch workflow for a large album

Drop the first 20 photos, press **Download all**, and attach that ZIP to the first email. Repeat for the next 20. Keeping the same target for every batch means each email ends up a similar, predictable size.
