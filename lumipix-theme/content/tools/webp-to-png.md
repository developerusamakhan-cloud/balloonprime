## When you need a PNG instead of a WebP

WebP is popular on websites because it makes images small, and it can store transparency. But many design apps, office programs and older editors still expect PNG when an image has a transparent background. You download a logo, an icon or a sticker, drop it into your project, and it either refuses to open or arrives with a solid block behind it.

PNG is the long-standing format for graphics with transparency. Converting the WebP to PNG gives you a file that keeps clean edges and see-through areas in almost any program. This tool does the conversion in your browser, so your files never leave your device.

## What a PNG keeps that a JPG would lose

If your image has transparency, PNG is the right target. Here is how the two choices compare for the same WebP.

| Feature | Save as PNG | Save as JPG |
|---|---|---|
| Transparent background | Kept | Filled with a background colour |
| Compression | Lossless, no new artefacts | Lossy, controlled by the **Quality** slider |
| File size | Usually larger | Usually smaller |
| Sharp text and flat colours | Stays crisp | Can show soft edges |
| Best for | Logos, icons, stickers, screenshots, graphics | Photos for sharing and printing |

If the image is a regular photo with no transparency, a JPG is often the better choice. Switch **Convert to** to JPG, or use the [WebP to JPG converter](tool:webp-to-jpg). For a deeper look at the two formats, see [PNG vs JPG](post:png-vs-jpg).

## Editing without extra quality loss

Every time you save a JPG, it is compressed again and a little detail is lost. PNG does not work that way. If you plan to crop, retouch, add text or combine an image with others, converting the WebP to PNG first gives you a stable working copy. You can make as many edits and saves as you like, then export a final JPG or WebP at the end if you need a smaller file.

Keep in mind that the conversion cannot repair the WebP. If the original was saved with lossy compression and has visible blocks or blur, the PNG keeps those exactly as they are. PNG protects the image from further loss; it does not add back detail that is already gone.

## Common problems and fixes

**The PNG is much larger than the WebP.** That is expected. PNG is lossless, so it cannot compress photos as tightly as WebP. For a photo without transparency, convert to JPG instead, or keep the WebP if your app supports it.

**The background is not transparent.** The original WebP did not contain transparency; converting cannot create it. To cut out a subject, use the [Background Remover](tool:background-remover), or remove a solid white backdrop with [Remove white background](tool:remove-white-background).

**An animation became a still image.** Only the first frame of an animated WebP is converted, because a PNG holds one picture.

**Colours shifted slightly.** Colours are converted to sRGB, the standard for screens and the web. The difference is rarely noticeable.

**A photo from an iPhone will not open.** HEIC is not supported in most browsers. Export the photo as JPG from the phone first.

## A simple workflow for logos and graphics

1. Save the WebP logo or graphic from the website or folder where you found it.

## When to keep the WebP

If the image is going straight back onto a website and your site builder accepts WebP, there is no need to convert. WebP files are smaller and load faster. Convert to PNG when you need compatibility with an app, a lossless working copy, or a format that a form or platform requires.
