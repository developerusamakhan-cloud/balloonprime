## Why WebP files cause trouble

Lots of websites serve images as WebP because the files are small. The trouble starts when you save one. An older photo viewer won't open it, an upload form rejects it, or an editing app throws an error.

JPG is the format almost everything understands. Turn the WebP into a JPG and you can open, print, attach, edit and upload it without worrying about whether it'll work.

## Which quality should you use?

The slider goes from 1 to 100. Higher keeps more detail and makes bigger files. Lower makes smaller files with more visible compression. Since a WebP is already compressed, there's not much point pushing it to 100.

| Quality | Good for |
|---|---|
| 90 to 100 | Photos you want to print or edit further |
| 80 to 90 | Everyday sharing, email and documents |
| 60 to 80 | Web uploads where file size matters |
| Under 60 | Only when you need a really small file |

If the JPG is bigger than you need, lower the quality and convert again, or use the [image compressor](tool:compress-image) to hit an exact size.

## What happens to transparency

WebP can have transparent areas, like a logo with no background or a sticker. JPG can't. When you save as JPG, those see-through pixels are filled with the **Background** colour you choose. White is safe for documents and forms. If the image will sit on a dark page or a coloured slide, pick that colour so the edges blend in.

Need to keep the transparency? Then JPG is the wrong target. You can [convert WebP to PNG](tool:webp-to-png) instead, which keeps transparent areas exactly as they are.

## Animated WebP files

Some WebP files are short animations, a bit like a GIF. A JPG can only hold one picture, so only the first frame is converted. If you want a different frame, take a screenshot of that moment and convert the screenshot.

## If something looks off

**The JPG is bigger than the WebP.** That's normal. WebP usually packs images more tightly than JPG, so the same picture often takes more space as a JPG. Lower the quality a little if size matters.

**The edges of a logo look white or blocky.** It had transparent areas that got filled with the background colour. Choose a background that matches where it'll be used, or convert to PNG.

**Only one frame of an animation came out.** JPG can't store animation, so you only get the first frame.

**Colours look slightly different.** Colours are converted to sRGB, the standard for screens and the web. Usually you can't see the change, but images with a wide colour profile may look a touch less vivid.

**A file won't open.** The tool opens anything your browser can read. HEIC photos from iPhones don't work in most browsers, so share those as JPG from the Photos app first.
