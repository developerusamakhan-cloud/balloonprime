## Why WebP files cause trouble

Many websites serve images as WebP because the files are small. The problem starts when you save one: you right-click a product photo or a wallpaper, and the file ends in .webp. Then an older photo viewer will not open it, an upload form rejects it, or an editing app shows an error.

JPG is the format almost everything understands. Turning a WebP into a JPG gives you a file you can open, print, attach, edit and upload without thinking about compatibility. This WebP to JPG converter does that on your own device, so the images are never sent anywhere.

## How to convert WebP to JPG

1. **Drop your WebP files** onto the tool, click to choose them, or paste an image with Ctrl+V. You can add up to 20 at once.

## Choosing the right quality

The **Quality** slider runs from 1 to 100. Higher values keep more detail and make bigger files; lower values make smaller files with more visible compression. Because a WebP is already compressed, there is little to gain from pushing the slider to 100.

| Quality | Best for |
|---|---|
| 90–100 | Photos you want to print or edit further |
| 80–90 | Everyday sharing, email and documents |
| 60–80 | Web uploads where file size matters |
| Below 60 | Only when you need a very small file |

After converting, compare the old and new sizes shown under each image. If the JPG is larger than you need, lower the quality and convert again, or run it through the [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image) to hit an exact target.

## What happens to transparency

WebP can store transparent areas, such as a logo with no background or a sticker. JPG cannot. When you save a WebP as JPG, any transparent pixels are filled with the **Background** colour you choose. White is the safe choice for documents and forms. If your image will sit on a dark page or a coloured slide, pick that colour instead so the edges blend in.

If you need to keep the transparency, a JPG is the wrong target. Switch **Convert to** to PNG, or use the [WebP to PNG converter](tool:webp-to-png), which keeps transparent areas intact.

## Animated WebP files

Some WebP files are short animations, like a GIF. A JPG can only hold a single picture, so only the first frame is converted. If you need a different frame, take a screenshot of the moment you want and convert that instead.

## Common problems and fixes

**The JPG is bigger than the WebP.** This is normal. WebP usually compresses more efficiently than JPG, so the same picture often takes more space as a JPG. Lower the **Quality** a little if size matters.

**The edges of a logo look white or blocky.** The image had transparent areas that were filled with the background colour. Choose a background that matches where the image will be used, or convert to PNG instead.

**Only one frame came out of an animation.** JPG cannot store animation, so only the first frame is saved.

**Colours look slightly different.** Colours are converted to sRGB, the standard colour space for screens and the web. In most cases the change is not visible; images that used a wide colour profile may look a little less saturated.

**A file will not open.** The tool opens anything your browser can decode. HEIC photos from iPhones are not supported in most browsers, so convert those on the phone first or share them as JPG from the Photos app.
