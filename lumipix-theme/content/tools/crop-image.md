Cropping is the quickest way to make a photo better. Cut off a messy edge, a stranger in the background or a big empty sky, and suddenly the picture is about your subject. These free crop image tools work right in your browser: drag the box, pick a shape, and save at full resolution. Your photo isn't uploaded.

## Cut away what you don't need

Cropping also changes the shape of an image, which is often exactly what a profile picture, a post or a form needs. You get a crop box you can move and resize with a mouse, a finger or the keyboard, standard aspect ratios, exact pixel sizes, and rotate and flip buttons.

## How to crop a photo

1. **Open your image.** The cropper works on one image at a time.
2. **Choose an aspect ratio** like **Free**, **1:1** or **16:9**, or type an exact width and height in pixels.
3. **Position the box.** Drag inside it to move it, and drag the corner handles to resize it. The arrow keys nudge it for fine tuning.
4. **Rotate or flip** if you need to, with **Rotate left**, **Rotate right** (90 degrees each) and **Flip horizontal**.
5. **Pick a format** under **Save as**: Same as original, JPG, PNG or WebP.
6. **Download** your cropped picture.

## Crop an image to a square

Square crops are the most common request, for profile photos, product images, listings and app icons. Tap **1:1**, then drag the box over your subject. For a portrait, leave a little room above the head and put the eyes about a third of the way down. For a logo you want to turn into a website icon, crop tight around the symbol and then open it in the [favicon to ICO](tool:favicon-generator) generator.

## Exact pixel sizes

The width and height fields show the crop size in pixels, and you can type into them too. Enter 1080 x 1080 or 1280 x 720 and the box snaps to that size. Then move it into place. Handy when a form or platform asks for specific dimensions and you want to choose exactly which part of the photo fills them.

The crop can't be bigger than the photo, because cropping only removes pixels. It never adds them. If the final image needs to be a bigger or smaller pixel size, crop for the shape first, then use the [image resizer](tool:image-resizer).

## Crop, resize or compress?

People mix these up all the time. Pick the one that matches what you're trying to change:

| You want to | Use | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Remove part of the picture or change its shape | This cropper | Pixels outside the box are removed |
| Make the whole picture smaller or bigger | The image resizer | Every pixel is scaled and nothing is cut off |
| Make the file smaller in KB or MB | The [image compressor](tool:compress-image) | The pixel size stays and the file gets lighter |

Often you need two in a row: crop to frame the subject, then resize or compress for an upload limit. For ID photos with a fixed print size, the [passport size photo creator](tool:passport-size-photo) crops and sets the DPI in one step.

## Rotate and flip

Phone photos sometimes turn up sideways after you copy them between devices. **Rotate left** and **Rotate right** turn the image in 90 degree steps so you can fix that before cropping. **Flip horizontal** mirrors the picture, which is useful for selfies that came out backwards, or when a person should face into a layout instead of out of it. Just remember that flipping mirrors any text in the photo too.

## Quality and formats

The crop is saved at full resolution. The pixels you keep are the original pixels, with no upscaling. **Same as original** keeps your file type. PNG is lossless and keeps transparency. JPG and WebP are smaller and go through normal compression when saved. For photos, JPG is usually the practical choice. For screenshots, logos and graphics with transparency, choose PNG. Our [PNG vs JPG](post:png-vs-jpg) guide explains the difference.

## If something's not right

- **The box won't get bigger.** It's hit the edge of the image at that ratio. Switch to **Free** or another ratio.
- **It's a few pixels off.** Nudge the box with the arrow keys, or type the exact width and height.
- **The result looks soft.** The area you kept was small to begin with. Cropping can't add detail, so start from the biggest original you have.
- **The photo is sideways.** Rotate it before you position the box.
- **Transparency turned white or black.** Save as PNG or WebP instead of JPG.

## Tips for a better crop

- **Follow the gaze.** Leave more space on the side a person is looking or moving towards.
- **Don't cut at the joints.** Avoid letting the edge fall right on wrists, elbows, knees or ankles.
- **Straighten first.** Rotate a sideways photo before you frame it, so the edges line up.
- **Always crop from the original.** Every crop and save of a JPG adds a little compression, so go back to the original for a new version.
- **Cropping for social media?** You can also [resize photo for Instagram](tool:resize-image-for-instagram) to get the exact size with Fit or Fill.
