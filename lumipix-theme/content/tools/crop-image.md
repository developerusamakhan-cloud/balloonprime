## Cut away what you do not need

Cropping is the quickest way to improve a photo. Removing a cluttered edge, a stranger in the background or an empty sky puts the attention back on your subject. It also changes the shape of an image, which is often what a profile picture, a post or a form needs. This cropper gives you a crop box you can move and resize with the mouse, finger or keyboard, a set of standard aspect ratios, exact pixel sizes, plus rotate and flip. Your photo is processed on your device and never uploaded.

## How to crop a photo

1. **Open your image.** The cropper works on one image at a time.
2. **Choose an aspect ratio**, such as **Free**, **1:1** or **16:9**, or type an exact width and height in pixels.
3. **Position the crop box.** Drag inside it to move it and drag the corner handles to resize it. Arrow keys nudge it for fine adjustments.
4. **Rotate or flip if needed** with **Rotate left**, **Rotate right** (90° each) and **Flip horizontal**.
5. **Pick a format** under **Save as**: Same as original, JPG, PNG or WebP.
6. **Download** your cropped picture.

## Crop image to square

Square crops are the most common request, for profile photos, product images, marketplace listings and app icons. Click **1:1**, then drag the box over the subject. For a portrait, leave a little space above the head and centre the eyes roughly a third of the way down. For a logo you plan to turn into a website icon, crop tightly around the symbol and then open it in the [Favicon generator](tool:favicon-generator).

## Exact pixel sizes

The width × height fields show the current crop size in pixels and also let you set it. Type 1080 × 1080 or 1280 × 720 and the box takes that size; then move it into position. This is useful when a form or platform asks for specific dimensions and you want to choose exactly which part of the photo fills them.

The crop size cannot be larger than the photo itself, because cropping only removes pixels; it never adds them. If you need the final image at a bigger or smaller pixel size, crop first for the shape, then use the [Image Resizer](tool:image-resizer).

## Crop, resize or compress?

These three are often confused. Pick the one that matches what you are trying to change:

| You want to | Use | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Remove part of the picture or change its shape | Crop image | Pixels outside the box are removed |
| Make the whole picture smaller or larger | [Image Resizer](tool:image-resizer) | Every pixel is scaled; nothing is cut off |
| Make the file smaller in KB or MB | [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image) | Pixel size stays; file size goes down |

Often you need two of them in a row: crop to frame the subject, then resize or compress for an upload limit.

## Rotate and flip

Phone photos sometimes appear sideways after copying them between devices. **Rotate left** and **Rotate right** turn the image in 90° steps so you can fix that before cropping. **Flip horizontal** mirrors the picture, which is handy for selfies that came out reversed or when a subject should face into a layout instead of out of it. Remember that flipping also mirrors any text in the photo.

## Quality and formats

The crop is saved at full resolution: the pixels you keep are the original pixels, with no upscaling. **Same as original** keeps your file type. PNG is lossless and keeps transparency; JPG and WebP are smaller and go through normal compression when saved. For photos, JPG is usually the practical choice; for screenshots, logos and graphics with transparency, choose PNG. Our [PNG vs JPG](post:png-vs-jpg) guide explains the difference.

## Common problems and fixes

- **The box will not get bigger.** It has reached the edge of the image at the chosen ratio. Switch to **Free** or a different ratio.
- **The crop is a few pixels off.** Use the arrow keys to nudge the box, or type the exact width and height.
- **The result looks soft.** The area you kept was small to begin with. Cropping cannot add detail, so start from the largest original you have.
- **The photo is sideways.** Rotate it before positioning the crop box.
- **Transparency turned white or black.** Save as PNG or WebP instead of JPG.

## Tips for a better crop

- **Follow the subject's gaze.** Leave more space in the direction a person is looking or moving.
- **Avoid cutting at joints.** Do not let the edge of the crop fall exactly on wrists, elbows, knees or ankles.
- **Straight lines first.** Rotate a sideways photo before you frame it, so edges line up.
- **Crop once from the original.** Repeated crops and saves of a JPG add compression each time, so go back to the original file for a new version.
