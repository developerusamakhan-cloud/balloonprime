## Full HD in one click

1920 x 1080 is also called Full HD or 1080p. It's the most common screen size around, and it's the standard for laptop and desktop wallpapers, presentation slides, video frames and TV slideshows.

## Fill or Fit?

Phone photos are usually 4:3, and portrait shots are tall, so neither matches 16:9.

- **Fill** cuts a strip off the top and bottom, or the sides, and covers the whole screen. Use it for wallpapers, slide backgrounds and thumbnails.
- **Fit** shows the whole photo with bars in a colour you pick. Use it for TV slideshows and portrait photos, where cropping would chop off heads.

Never stretch. A stretched photo makes faces and buildings look wrong, and people notice straight away.

## Portrait photos on a wide screen

A tall photo inside a wide frame leaves big empty spaces on either side. Make them look deliberate: choose **Fit** and use a colour from the photo, like sky blue or a warm beige, or go with black for a cinema look.

3. Tap **Download all** and copy the folder onto a USB stick.
4. Open the folder in the TV's media player.

Most TVs, 4K ones included, scale Full HD images up smoothly.

A 1920 x 1080 JPG is usually somewhere between 300KB and 1MB. That's fine for wallpapers and slides. For a banner on a website, [compress image to 500KB](tool:compress-image-to-500kb) or smaller, or save it as WebP if your site supports it.

## Starting from a small photo

If the original is narrower than 1920 pixels, it gets enlarged. A small step up looks fine. A big one looks soft, because enlarging can't add detail. Use the biggest original you've got.

## Questions people ask about Full HD images

**Will 1920 x 1080 look sharp on a 4K TV?** For photos you watch from the sofa, yes. TVs scale Full HD smoothly. For a 4K monitor you sit close to, use the 4K preset.

**JPG or PNG?** JPG for photos, which keeps files small. PNG for slides and graphics with text and flat colours.

**Can I make a wallpaper across two monitors?** Turn the link off, type 3840 x 1080 for two Full HD screens side by side, then choose Fill.

**What about phone wallpapers?** Phones are tall, not wide. Use a portrait size like 1080 x 1920 in the image resizer.

**Does resizing make it worse?** Making a big photo smaller keeps it sharp. It's only enlarging a small photo that can make it look soft.

## Presentation slides

PowerPoint and Google Slides use 16:9 by default, which matches 1920 x 1080 exactly. Resize background photos to this size with **Fill**, and leave a calm area for your titles. The photo then fills the slide edge to edge without any stretching.
