## Full HD in one click

1920×1080, also called Full HD or 1080p, is the most common screen resolution. It is the standard for laptop and desktop wallpapers, presentation slides, video frames and TV slideshows. This tool resizes any photo to exactly 1920×1080 without stretching it, and it runs in your browser, so your photos are never uploaded.

## How to resize to 1920×1080

1. **Drop your photo**, or up to 20 photos for a slideshow.
2. The **Full HD 1920×1080** size is already set. Other screen sizes are one tap away.
3. **Choose Fill** to crop the photo to 16:9, or **Fit** to keep the whole photo with a background colour.
4. **Download** one file or click **Download all**.

## Fill or Fit?

Most phone photos are 4:3 and portrait photos are tall, so they do not match 16:9.

- **Fill – crop edges** removes a strip from the top and bottom (or the sides) and fills the screen. Use it for wallpapers, slide backgrounds and thumbnails.
- **Fit – add background** shows the whole photo with bars in a colour you choose. Use it for TV slideshows and portrait photos, where cropping would cut off faces.

Avoid stretching. A stretched photo distorts faces and buildings, and viewers notice immediately.

## Portrait photos on a widescreen

A tall photo inside a wide frame leaves large empty areas on both sides. Make them look intentional: pick **Fit** and choose a colour taken from the photo, such as a sky blue or a warm beige, or use black for a cinematic look. For a more designed result, place two portrait photos side by side in an editor.

## Slideshows on a TV

1. Drop up to 20 photos.
2. Keep the 1920×1080 size and choose **Fit** with a black background.
3. Click **Download all** and copy the folder to a USB stick.
4. Open the folder on the TV's media player.

Most TVs, including 4K models, scale Full HD images smoothly.

## File size

A 1920×1080 JPG is usually between 300KB and 1MB. That is fine for wallpapers and slides. For a website hero image, compress it further to around 200–300KB with the [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image), or choose WebP if your site supports it.

## Starting from a small photo

If the original is narrower than 1920 pixels, it will be enlarged. Small increases look fine, but large ones look soft, because enlarging cannot add detail. Use the largest original you have.

## Common questions about Full HD images

**Will a 1920×1080 image look sharp on a 4K TV?** Yes for photos viewed from the sofa. TVs scale Full HD smoothly. For a 4K monitor viewed up close, use the 4K preset instead.

**Should I use JPG or PNG?** JPG for photos, which keeps files small. PNG for slides and graphics with text and flat colours.

**Can I make a wallpaper for two monitors?** Turn the link off and type 3840 × 1080 for two Full HD screens side by side, then choose Fill.

**What about phone wallpapers?** Phones are tall, not wide. Use a portrait size such as 1080 × 1920 in the [Image Resizer](tool:image-resizer) instead.

**Does resizing reduce quality?** Making a large photo smaller keeps it sharp. Only enlarging a small photo can make it look soft.

## Presentation slides

PowerPoint and Google Slides use a 16:9 layout by default, which matches 1920×1080 exactly. Resize background photos to this size with **Fill**, and leave a calm area for titles. The result fills the slide edge to edge without stretching.
