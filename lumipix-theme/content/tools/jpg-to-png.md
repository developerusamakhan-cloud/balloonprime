## What converting to PNG does, and what it doesn't

Worth knowing before you start: converting a JPG to PNG changes the file format, not the picture. The PNG holds exactly the same pixels the JPG had. It won't look sharper, and it won't fix any blockiness that's already there. Because PNG is lossless, the file is usually bigger too.

So why bother? Because a PNG stops the image getting any worse. Every time a JPG is saved again, it's compressed again and loses a little detail. A PNG can be edited and saved as often as you like with no extra loss. And some forms, apps and design tools simply insist on PNG.

## Handy tips

- **Convert before you edit, not after.** If you're going to make several rounds of changes, edit the PNG and only export a JPG at the very end.
- **Start from the best JPG you've got.** The PNG can't be better than its source, so use the original rather than a copy from a chat app.
- **Mix formats in one batch.** It also opens WebP, AVIF, GIF, BMP, ICO and SVG.
- **Resize if the PNG is too heavy.** Big photos as PNG can be large files. Make them smaller in the [image resizer](tool:image-resizer) and save as PNG there.
- **Watch out for size limits.** If a form wants PNG and has a strict size limit, fewer pixels is the best way to get there.

## If something's not right

**The PNG is several times bigger than the JPG.** That's how PNG handles photos. It stores every pixel without lossy compression. Make it smaller in pixels, or check whether JPG is accepted after all.

**It doesn't look any sharper.** Converting can't add detail. A blurry JPG makes a blurry PNG.

**The background is still there.** Converting doesn't cut anything out. Use a background removal tool afterwards.

**The form still rejects it.** Check for a size or dimension limit. A PNG photo can be over the limit even when the format is right.

**An iPhone photo won't open.** Most browsers can't read HEIC. Share it from the phone as JPG first, then convert.

## An editing routine that doesn't lose quality

1. Convert the original JPG to PNG here.
2. Do all your cropping, retouching, text and notes on the PNG, saving as often as you need.
