## What converting to PNG does and does not do

It helps to be clear about this before you start. Converting a JPG to PNG changes the file format, not the picture. The PNG holds exactly the same pixels the JPG already had, with no extra sharpness and no repaired compression artefacts. Because PNG is lossless, the file is usually larger than the JPG.

So why convert at all? Because a PNG stops the image from getting any worse. Every time a JPG is saved again, it is compressed again and loses a little detail. A PNG can be edited and saved as many times as you like with no further loss. Some upload forms, apps and design tools also simply require PNG. This JPG to PNG converter handles all of that on your device, with nothing uploaded.

## What changes and what stays the same

| Property | After converting to PNG |
|---|---|
| Pixel dimensions | Unchanged |
| Visible quality | Same as the JPG; no improvement |
| Existing JPG artefacts | Kept exactly as they were |
| Future saves | No further compression loss |
| File size | Usually larger |
| Background | Stays as it is; not made transparent |

## PNG does not remove the background

A common expectation is that converting to PNG will make the background transparent. It will not. PNG can store transparency, but the conversion keeps whatever is in the photo, including a white wall or a coloured backdrop. To make the background see-through, convert here if you need a PNG to work with, then use the [Background Remover](tool:background-remover) for photos, or [Remove white background](tool:remove-white-background) for logos and signatures on white paper. Both download a transparent PNG.

## Practical tips

- **Convert before you edit, not after.** If you will make several rounds of changes, convert to PNG first and edit the PNG. Export a JPG only at the very end if you need a smaller file.
- **Start from the best JPG you have.** The PNG cannot be better than its source, so use the original photo rather than a copy downloaded from a chat app.
- **Mix formats in one batch.** The tool also opens WebP, AVIF, GIF, BMP, ICO and SVG.
- **Resize if the PNG is too heavy.** Large photos as PNG can be big. Reduce the pixel size with the [Image Resizer](tool:image-resizer) and save as PNG there.
- **Check the size limit.** If a form needs PNG and has a strict size limit, a smaller pixel size is the most effective way to get there.

## Common problems and fixes

**The PNG is several times larger than the JPG.** This is how PNG works with photos. It stores every pixel without lossy compression. Reduce the dimensions if the size is a problem, or ask whether JPG is accepted.

**The image does not look any sharper.** Converting cannot add detail. If the JPG is blurry or blocky, the PNG will look the same.

**The background is still there.** PNG conversion does not cut anything out. Use a background removal tool after converting.

**The upload form still rejects the file.** Check whether it also has a size or dimension limit. A PNG photo may exceed it even when the format is right.

**An iPhone photo will not open.** HEIC is not supported in most browsers. Share or export it from the phone as JPG, then convert.

## A lossless editing workflow

1. Convert the original JPG to PNG here.
2. Do all your cropping, retouching, text and annotations on the PNG, saving as often as you need.
3. When you are finished, keep the PNG as your master copy.
4. If you need a small file to share, convert the final PNG to JPG once.

This way, the photo is compressed as JPG only twice in its life: when the camera took it and when you export the final version.

## JPG or PNG for a signature or logo?

Signatures and logos photographed or scanned as JPG often need a transparent background for documents and websites. Convert the file, remove the white paper behind it, and keep the result as PNG. A JPG would bring the white box straight back.
