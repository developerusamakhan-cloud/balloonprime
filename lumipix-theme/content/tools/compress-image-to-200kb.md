## Plenty of room for documents

200KB is a comfortable limit. It's the size many portals pick for scanned documents: ID cards, degrees, transcripts, domicile certificates, experience letters and fee receipts. A full A4 page stays readable at around 1500 to 2000 pixels wide, and photos look sharp even on a big screen.

## Get a good scan first

How you scan the page matters more than anything else for readability:

- **Use your phone's built-in scanner.** On iPhone, the Notes app has **Scan Documents**. On Android, Google Drive has a **Scan** button. Both straighten and crop the page for you.
- **Lay the page flat** on a dark surface in daylight, with no shadow across it.
- **Crop to the edges** of the page or card.
- **Add a little contrast** if the text looks faint.

Then bring the result here and compress it.

## Photos at 200KB

For a photo, 200KB is generous. A portrait can be 1600 to 2000 pixels wide at good quality, which looks great on any screen. It's a sensible size for profile pictures on professional sites, product photos in a small online shop, and photos you want to email without huge attachments.

## Can you actually read it?

Open each compressed file and zoom to 100%. Can you read registration numbers, dates and names without squinting? If not, try one of these:

- scan it again in better light
- crop tighter
- set a **Maximum width** of about 1600 pixels under **Advanced options**, so the detail goes into the text instead of a huge, empty page

## Name your files properly

Before you upload, rename the files so you can't mix them up: `cnic-front.jpg`, `cnic-back.jpg`, `degree.jpg`, `transcript-page-1.jpg`. Keep the originals in a separate folder. When another form asks for the same documents with a different limit, compress the originals again, not the copies you already shrank.

## Where 200KB comes up

**Scholarship applications** usually want transcripts, certificates and income documents. Each page at 200KB stays readable and the total upload stays manageable.

**Visa and immigration forms** in some countries accept supporting documents as images with a size limit. Compress each page and check that stamps and handwritten details are still clear.

**Insurance and bank forms** sometimes ask for photos of documents or damage. 200KB keeps the details visible without a slow upload.

**Small online shops** can use 200KB as a target for product photos. They look sharp and pages still load fast.

## Colour or black and white?

Use colour for ID cards, certificates with coloured seals, and anything where colour means something. For typed letters and transcripts, a greyscale copy often looks cleaner and leaves more of the 200KB for sharp text. Most phone editors have a black and white filter. Apply it before you compress.
