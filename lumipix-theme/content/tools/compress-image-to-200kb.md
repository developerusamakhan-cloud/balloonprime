A 200KB limit gives you plenty of room. It is the size many portals choose for scanned documents: identity cards, degrees, transcripts, domicile certificates, experience letters and fee receipts. At 200KB, a full A4 page stays readable at around 1500–2000 pixels wide, and photos look sharp even on large screens.

## Scan documents properly first

A good scan makes the biggest difference to readability:

- **Use your phone's document scanner.** On iPhone, the Notes app has **Scan Documents**. On Android, Google Drive has a **Scan** option. Both straighten and crop the page for you.
- **Lay the page flat** on a dark surface, in even daylight, without shadows.
- **Crop to the edges** of the page or card.
- **Increase contrast slightly** if text looks faint.

Then compress the result here.

## Photos at 200KB

For photos, 200KB is generous. A portrait can be 1600–2000 pixels wide at good quality, which looks excellent on any screen. Use this size for profile pictures on professional sites, product photos on small shops, and images in emails where you want quality without huge attachments.

## Readability check before uploading

Open each compressed file and zoom to 100%. Can you read registration numbers, dates and names without squinting? If not:

- re-scan with better light,
- crop more tightly, or
- set a **Maximum width** around 1600 pixels under **Advanced options**, so the compressor keeps detail in the text rather than spreading it over a very large canvas.

## Several pages in one upload

Some portals want all pages of a document in a single file, usually a PDF. If the portal accepts separate images instead, compress each page to the limit. If there is a total limit for all pages together, divide it between them: for example, four pages under a 600KB total can each be compressed to about 150KB.

## File naming tips

Before uploading, rename files so you cannot mix them up: `cnic-front.jpg`, `cnic-back.jpg`, `degree.jpg`, `transcript-page-1.jpg`. Keep the originals in a separate folder. When another form asks for the same documents with a different limit, compress the originals again rather than the already compressed copies.

## Common situations

**Scholarship applications** often ask for transcripts, certificates and income documents. Each page scanned and compressed to 200KB stays readable while keeping the total upload manageable.

**Visa and immigration forms** in some countries accept supporting documents as images with a file size limit. Compress each page and check that stamps and handwritten details remain clear.

**Insurance and bank forms** sometimes request photos of documents or damage. 200KB keeps details visible without slow uploads.

**Small online shops** can use 200KB as a sensible target for product photos: sharp on screens, light enough for fast pages.

## Colour vs greyscale

Colour scans are best for ID cards, certificates with coloured seals, and anything where colour carries meaning. For typed letters and transcripts, a greyscale version often looks cleaner and leaves more of the 200KB budget for sharp text. Most phone editors have a black-and-white filter; apply it before compressing.
