## One PDF instead of a pile of photos

Lots of forms, offices and people you email want one PDF, not ten separate pictures. This tool puts your images into one file, one image per page, in the order you choose. Each image is scaled to fit the page and centred, and it's never stretched, so documents keep their natural shape.

## How to turn JPG into a PDF

1. **Drop your images** onto the page or click to pick them. Up to 20 JPG, PNG, WebP or AVIF files.
2. **Put the pages in order.** Thumbnails appear in a list. Use the up and down arrows or drag them around, and click x to remove one.
3. **Choose your options:** **Page size**, **Orientation**, **Margin** and **Quality**.
4. **Click Create PDF.**
5. **That's it.** The PDF is built on your device and downloads straight away.

The first image in the list becomes page 1, so put the front of the ID card or the first page of the letter at the top.

## What the options do

| Option | Choices | When to use them |
|---|---|---|
| **Page size** | A4, US Letter, Same as image | A4 or US Letter for printing and forms. Same as image for a PDF that matches the photo exactly |
| **Orientation** | Auto, Portrait, Landscape | Auto follows each image. Pick Portrait or Landscape to make every page the same |
| **Margin** | None, Small, Large | None to fill the page. Small or Large for a white border that prints neatly |
| **Quality** | High, Balanced, Smaller file | High for detail, Balanced for everyday use, Smaller file for email and upload limits |

Auto orientation is handy for mixed sets. A landscape photo of a whiteboard gets a landscape page and a tall receipt gets a portrait page, all in the same PDF.

## Better PDFs from phone photos

The PDF can only be as clear as the photos you put in. A few habits help a lot:

- **Use good, even light.** Daylight from a window is ideal. Watch out for your own shadow falling across the page.
- **Hold the phone straight above the page** so the lines of text are level and the corners are square.
- **Lay the paper flat** on a dark, plain surface so the page edges are easy to see.
- **Crop off the table.** Use the [crop image tools](tool:crop-image) to trim the background before you make the PDF. They can also turn a sideways photo by 90 degrees.
- **Check each thumbnail** before you click **Create PDF**, and remove any blurry shots.

## Keeping the PDF small enough to send

## If something's not right

**The pages are in the wrong order.** Move the thumbnails with the arrows or by dragging, then click **Create PDF** again.

**A page is sideways.** **Orientation** sets the page, not the image. If the photo itself was taken sideways, rotate it in the crop tool first, then add it here.

**There are white borders around the image.** The image shape doesn't match the paper. Choose **Same as image** for page size, or set **Margin** to None.

**Transparent areas look white.** That's normal. Transparent parts of PNG or WebP images become white in the PDF.

**I can't select or search the text.** The PDF contains the images as pictures. Text in photos isn't turned into selectable text, because there's no OCR.

**My iPhone photo won't load.** Most browsers can't read HEIC. Export the photo as JPG first.

**The PDF is too big.** Choose Smaller file, or remove pages you don't need.

## Putting an ID card into one PDF

1. Photograph the front and back of the card on a plain, dark surface.
2. Crop each photo close to the card edges.
3. Drop both images here and make sure the front comes first.
4. Choose A4, Auto orientation and a Small margin.
5. Click **Create PDF** and attach the file to your form or email.
