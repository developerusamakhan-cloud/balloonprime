Turn photos, scans and screenshots into a single, tidy document with this free image to pdf converter that runs entirely in your browser.

## One PDF instead of a pile of photos

Many forms, offices and email recipients want one PDF, not ten separate pictures. Perhaps you photographed a multi-page letter, the front and back of an ID card, a stack of receipts or your homework. This tool puts those images into one PDF, one image per page, in the order you choose. Each image is scaled to fit the page and centred, and it is never stretched, so documents keep their natural proportions.

## How to convert images to PDF

1. **Drop your images** onto the page or click to choose them. You can add up to 20 JPG, PNG, WebP or AVIF files.
2. **Arrange the pages.** Thumbnails appear in a list. Use the up and down arrows or drag and drop to change the order, and click × to remove an image.
3. **Choose your options:** **Page size**, **Orientation**, **Margin** and **Quality**.
4. Click **Create PDF**.
5. The PDF is built on your device and downloads directly.

The first image in the list becomes page 1, so put the front of an ID card or the first page of a letter at the top.

## The options explained

| Option | Choices | When to use each |
|---|---|---|
| **Page size** | A4, US Letter, Same as image | A4 or US Letter for printing and forms; Same as image for a PDF that matches the photo exactly |
| **Orientation** | Auto, Portrait, Landscape | Auto follows each image; pick Portrait or Landscape to make every page the same |
| **Margin** | None, Small, Large | None to fill the page; Small or Large for a white border that prints cleanly |
| **Quality** | High, Balanced, Smaller file | High for detail; Balanced for everyday use; Smaller file for email and upload limits |

**Orientation** set to Auto is useful for mixed sets. A landscape photo of a whiteboard gets a landscape page, while a portrait receipt gets a portrait page, all in the same PDF.

## Getting good results from phone photos

The PDF can only be as clear as the photos you put in. A few habits make a big difference:

- **Use good, even light.** Daylight from a window works well. Avoid casting your own shadow over the page.
- **Hold the phone straight above the page,** so the text lines are level and the corners are square.
- **Lay paper flat** on a dark, plain surface so the edges of the page are easy to see.
- **Crop away the table.** Use the [Crop image](tool:crop-image) tool to trim the background before creating the PDF. It can also rotate a sideways photo by 90°.
- **Check each thumbnail** in the list before clicking **Create PDF**, and remove any blurry shots with ×.

## Keeping the PDF small enough to send

Email services and online portals often limit attachment size. The **Quality** option is the simplest control: choose Smaller file when the PDF only needs to be readable on screen. If your photos are very large, you can also reduce them first with the [Image Resizer](tool:image-resizer) or the [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image). ## Common problems and fixes

**The pages are in the wrong order.** Move the thumbnails with the arrows or by dragging, then click **Create PDF** again.

**A page is sideways.** **Orientation** controls the page, not the image. If the photo itself was taken sideways, rotate it in the [Crop image](tool:crop-image) tool first, then add it here.

**There are white borders around the image.** The image's shape does not match the paper. Choose **Page size** Same as image, or set **Margin** to None.

**Transparent areas look white.** That is expected: transparent parts of PNG or WebP images become white in the PDF.

**I cannot select or search the text.** The PDF contains the images as pictures. Text in photos is not converted into selectable text, because there is no OCR.

**My iPhone photo will not load.** HEIC photos open only in browsers that can decode them, which most browsers cannot. Convert or export the photo as JPG first.

**The PDF is too big.** Choose **Quality** Smaller file, or remove pages you do not need.

## Combining an ID card into one PDF

1. Photograph the front and back of the card on a plain, dark surface.
2. Crop each photo close to the card edges.
3. Drop both images here and make sure the front is first.
4. Choose A4, **Orientation** Auto and a Small **Margin**.
5. Click **Create PDF** and attach the file to your form or email.

## Before you upload to a form

Many application portals state both a file type and a maximum size. Check whether the form wants one PDF or separate images, and how many KB it allows.
