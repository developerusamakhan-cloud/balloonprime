## Why turn a PNG into a JPG?

PNG is lossless, which is great for screenshots, logos and graphics. It also means photos saved as PNG are often huge. A phone screenshot, a scanned document or a photo exported from an editor can easily be several times bigger than the same picture as a JPG.

That causes real headaches: upload forms that only take JPG, size limits on job portals and exam sites, attachments that won't send, and slow web pages. Converting to JPG usually fixes all of them at once.

## How to turn PNG to JPG

1. **Drop your PNG files**, click to pick them, or paste a screenshot straight from the clipboard with Ctrl+V. Up to 20 at a time.
2. **Convert to** is already set to JPG.
3. **Adjust Quality** if you need to. The default is 92.
4. **Choose a Background colour** for any transparent areas. White is the default.
5. **Compare the sizes** shown for each file, then download them one by one or tap **Download all** for a ZIP.

## Should this PNG become a JPG?

Not every PNG benefits. Here's a quick guide:

| Image | Convert to JPG? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A photo saved as PNG | Yes | Usually much smaller as JPG |
| A scanned document or ID card | Yes | Forms usually want JPG and a small file |
| A screenshot of a photo or video | Yes | Smaller file, looks the same |
| A screenshot of text or code | Maybe | JPG can soften letter edges, so use high quality |
| A logo with a transparent background | No | JPG can't store transparency |
| An icon or graphic with flat colours | Usually not | The PNG may already be small and crisper |

For the full comparison, read [PNG vs JPG](post:png-vs-jpg).

## Transparent areas and the background colour

JPG has no transparency. If your PNG has see-through parts, the tool fills them with the **Background** colour you pick before saving. That's actually handy when you know where the image is going.

| Where the JPG is going | Background to choose |
|---|---|
| Documents, forms, printing | White |
| A dark website or slide | Black, or the matching dark colour |
| A coloured slide template | The slide's background colour |
| A product listing on a light page | White, or the page colour |

If you need to keep the transparency, leave it as PNG, or [convert PNG to WebP](tool:png-to-webp), which keeps transparency with much smaller files.

## Getting the size you need

The **Quality** slider goes from 1 to 100. For most photos and scans, anything from 80 to 92 gives a big drop in size with no visible change. Screenshots full of text look best at 90 or above.

If a form wants a strict limit like 50KB or 100KB, convert to JPG first, then use the [image compressor](tool:compress-image) or a set size like [compress image to 100KB](tool:compress-image-to-100kb). Starting from a JPG makes small targets easier to reach.

## If something's not right

**There's a white box where the background was transparent.** That's the background fill. Choose a colour that matches where the image will appear, or keep it as PNG.

**Text in a screenshot looks fuzzy.** JPG softens sharp edges. Raise the quality to 95 or more, or keep text-heavy screenshots as PNG.

**The JPG isn't much smaller.** Simple graphics with flat colours are already efficient as PNG. Converting helps most with photos, scans and detailed screenshots.

**It's still too big for the form.** Lower the quality a little, make it smaller in pixels, or use a target size compressor.

**An iPhone HEIC photo won't open.** Most browsers can't read HEIC. Export it as JPG from the phone first.

## Getting a scan ready for an online form

1. Scan or photograph the document and save it as PNG, or paste the scan here.
2. Keep **Background** on white and set **Quality** to around 85.
3. Download the JPG and check the new size under the result.
4. If it's over the form's limit, [compress image to 200KB](tool:compress-image-to-200kb) or whatever the limit is, then upload.

## When PNG is still the better choice

Keep PNG for logos, icons, line drawings, transparent graphics and anything you'll edit again. Every JPG save adds a little compression, so keep the PNG as your master copy and only convert to JPG for the final upload or share.
