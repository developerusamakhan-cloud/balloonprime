Turn PNG to JPG here to make large screenshots, scans and photos into smaller files that every website and app accepts.

## Why turn a PNG into a JPG

PNG is lossless, which makes it ideal for screenshots, logos and graphics. It also means that photos saved as PNG are often very large. A phone screenshot, a scanned document or a photo exported from an editor can easily be several times the size of the same picture as a JPG.

That causes real problems: upload forms that only accept JPG, size limits on job portals and exam sites, email attachments that will not send, and slow-loading pages. Converting to JPG usually solves all of these at once. This PNG to JPG converter runs in your browser, so nothing is uploaded.

## How to convert PNG to JPG

2. **Convert to** is already set to JPG.
3. Adjust **Quality** if needed. The default is 92.
4. Choose a **Background** colour for transparent areas. White is the default.
5. Compare the old size and new size shown for each file, then download them one by one or use **Download all** for a ZIP.

## Should this PNG become a JPG?

Not every PNG benefits from conversion. Use this table as a quick guide.

| Image | Convert to JPG? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Photo saved as PNG | Yes | Usually much smaller as JPG |
| Scanned document or ID card | Yes | Forms usually want JPG and a small file |
| Screenshot of a photo or video | Yes | Smaller file, looks the same |
| Screenshot of text or code | Maybe | JPG can blur letter edges slightly; use high quality |
| Logo with a transparent background | No | JPG cannot store transparency |
| Icon or graphic with few flat colours | Usually no | PNG may already be small and crisper |

For the full comparison, read [PNG vs JPG](post:png-vs-jpg).

## Transparent areas and the Background colour

JPG has no transparency. If your PNG has see-through areas, the tool fills them with the **Background** colour you choose before saving. This is useful rather than a limitation when you know where the image is going.

| Where the JPG will be used | Background to choose |
|---|---|
| Documents, forms, printing | White |
| Dark website or dark slide | Black or the matching dark colour |
| Coloured presentation template | The slide's background colour |
| Product listing on a light page | White or the page colour |

If you need the transparency, keep the file as PNG, or switch **Convert to** to WebP, which keeps transparency with much smaller files. The [PNG to WebP](tool:png-to-webp) page is set up for that.

## Getting the size you need

The **Quality** slider goes from 1 to 100. For most photos and scans, values between 80 and 92 give a big drop in file size with no visible change. Text-heavy screenshots look best at 90 or above.

If a form asks for a strict limit such as 50KB or 100KB, convert to JPG first, then use the [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image) or a fixed-size tool like [compress to 100KB](tool:compress-image-to-100kb). Converting first means the compressor starts from a JPG, which makes reaching small targets easier.

## Common problems and fixes

**The JPG has a white box where the background was transparent.** That is the **Background** fill. Choose a different colour that matches where the image will appear, or keep the file as PNG.

**Text in a screenshot looks fuzzy.** JPG compression softens sharp edges. Raise **Quality** to 95 or higher, or keep text-heavy screenshots as PNG.

**The JPG is not much smaller.** Simple graphics with flat colours are already efficient as PNG. Conversion helps most with photos, scans and detailed screenshots.

**The file is still too big for the form.** Lower the quality a little, reduce the pixel dimensions, or use a target-size compressor.

**An iPhone HEIC photo will not open.** Most browsers cannot decode HEIC. Export it as JPG from the phone first.

## When PNG is still the better choice

Keep PNG for logos, icons, line drawings, transparent graphics and anything you will edit again. Each JPG save adds a little compression, so for working files it is best to keep the PNG as your master copy and convert to JPG only for the final upload or share.
