## Print sizes without the maths

Printers, photo labs and many official forms describe sizes in centimetres, millimetres or inches, while digital images are measured in pixels. The link between them is DPI, the number of pixels in each inch of paper. This tool does the conversion for you: choose a print size, keep the DPI at 300, and download a JPG with the right number of pixels and the DPI written into the file.

Your photos are processed in your browser and never uploaded.

## How to resize for print

1. **Drop your photo** or tap **Choose files**.
2. **Tap a preset** such as 10×15 cm, 4×6 in, 5×7 in, A5 or A4, or type your own size.
3. **Pick the unit**: cm, mm or inch. Set the **DPI** (300 for photos).
4. **Choose Fill** to fill the print exactly, or **Fit** to keep the whole photo with a border.
5. **Download** the JPG and print it at **100% scale**.

The hint under the controls shows the resulting pixel size, so you can check it before downloading.

| A5 | 210 × 148 mm | 2480 × 1748 (landscape) |
| A4 | 297 × 210 mm | 3508 × 2480 (landscape) |
| Passport | 35 × 45 mm | 413 × 531 |

The presets are landscape. For a portrait print, swap the width and height values.

## Why DPI matters

The same photo can print at very different sizes depending on its DPI. A 1800×1200 image prints at 6×4 inches at 300 DPI, or 12×8 inches at 150 DPI. At 300 DPI, prints held in the hand look sharp. Posters viewed from a distance look fine at 150 DPI. When you save as JPG here, the DPI is stored in the file, so print software uses the size you chose instead of guessing.

Our [print size guide](post:image-size-in-inches-print-guide) has a full chart, and [how to check if a photo is 300 DPI](post:how-to-check-if-photo-is-300-dpi) explains where to see the value on Windows and Mac.

## Fill or Fit for prints?

Most photos are 4:3, while a 10×15 cm print is 3:2. The shapes differ, so something has to give:

- **Fill – crop edges** fills the paper completely and trims a little from the top and bottom (or sides). Best for most photos.
- **Fit – add background** keeps the whole picture and adds a white border. Best for group photos where nobody should be cut off.

Choosing yourself avoids surprises from the print lab's automatic cropping.

## Does my photo have enough pixels?

Divide the pixel width by the print width in inches. If the result is 300 or more, the print will be sharp. A typical 12-megapixel phone photo (about 4000×3000 pixels) is enough for an A4 print at 300 DPI. If your photo is smaller, it will be enlarged; that works for modest increases but cannot add real detail.

## Printing at home

Choose photo paper in your printer settings and set the scale to **100%** or "actual size". Avoid "fit to page", which ignores the size you chose. For several small photos on one sheet, place them on a larger canvas in a photo editor.

## Documents and scans

The same tool works for documents. Set **mm** and choose A4 or A5 with **Fit** and a white background to keep the whole page. For uploading the result to a form with a file size limit, compress it afterwards with the [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image), which keeps the DPI value.

## Quick conversions

- 1 inch = 2.54 cm = 25.4 mm
- At 300 DPI, 1 cm ≈ 118 pixels and 1 inch = 300 pixels
- At 150 DPI, 1 cm ≈ 59 pixels and 1 inch = 150 pixels

The tool applies these automatically. Switching the unit converts the current numbers, so the print size stays the same while you change between cm, mm and inches.
