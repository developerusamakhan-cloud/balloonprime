Photo labs and printers talk in centimetres and inches. Your phone talks in pixels. This page sorts out the gap: pick a print size, keep the DPI at 300, and download a JPG that prints at exactly that size. You can resize an image into inches, cm or mm without doing any maths, and your photos aren't uploaded anywhere.

## Print sizes without the maths

The link between pixels and paper is DPI, which is how many pixels fit in each inch. Choose your size, and the tool works out the pixels and writes the DPI into the file. Print software then knows the size you meant instead of guessing.

## How to resize an image for print

1. **Drop your photo** or tap **Choose files**.
2. **Tap a preset** like 10 x 15 cm, 4 x 6 in, 5 x 7 in, A5 or A4, or type your own size.
3. **Pick the unit** (cm, mm or inch) and set the **DPI**. Use 300 for photos.
4. **Choose Fill** to cover the print exactly, or **Fit** to keep the whole photo with a border.
5. **Download** the JPG and print it at **100% scale**.

The line under the controls shows the final size in pixels, so you can check before you download.

## Common print sizes at 300 DPI

| Print | Size | Pixels |
|---|---|---|
| Standard photo | 10 x 15 cm | 1772 x 1181 (landscape) |
| US standard | 4 x 6 in | 1800 x 1200 (landscape) |
| Medium | 5 x 7 in | 2100 x 1500 (landscape) |
| A5 | 210 x 148 mm | 2480 x 1748 (landscape) |
| A4 | 297 x 210 mm | 3508 x 2480 (landscape) |
| Passport | 35 x 45 mm | 413 x 531 |

The presets are landscape. For a portrait print, just swap the width and height.

## Why DPI matters

The same photo can print at very different sizes depending on its DPI. An 1800 x 1200 image prints at 6 x 4 inches at 300 DPI, or at 12 x 8 inches at 150 DPI. Prints you hold in your hand look sharp at 300. Posters you look at from across the room are fine at 150.

## Fill or Fit?

Most phone photos are 4:3, but a 10 x 15 cm print is 3:2. The shapes don't match, so something has to give:

- **Fill** covers the whole paper and trims a little off the top and bottom, or the sides. Best for most photos.
- **Fit** keeps the whole picture and adds a white border. Best for group shots where nobody should lose their head.

Choosing yourself saves you from the lab's automatic cropping.

## Does my photo have enough pixels?

Divide the width in pixels by the print width in inches. If you get 300 or more, the print will be sharp. A normal 12 megapixel phone photo, about 4000 x 3000 pixels, is enough for an A4 print at 300 DPI. Smaller photos get enlarged. That's fine for a small step up, but it can't add detail that isn't there.

If you only want to change the DPI number without touching the pixels, use the page to [change image DPI](tool:change-image-dpi) instead.

## Printing at home

In your printer settings, choose photo paper and set the scale to **100%** or "actual size". Avoid "fit to page", because it ignores the size you chose. For ID photos, the [passport size photo creator](tool:passport-size-photo) can put several copies on one sheet with cut lines.

## Handy conversions

- 1 inch = 2.54 cm = 25.4 mm
- At 300 DPI, 1 cm is about 118 pixels and 1 inch is 300 pixels
- At 150 DPI, 1 cm is about 59 pixels and 1 inch is 150 pixels

The tool does this for you. If you switch units, the numbers are converted so the print size stays the same while you flip between cm, mm and inches. For sizes in pixels and percent, use the main [image resizer](tool:image-resizer).
