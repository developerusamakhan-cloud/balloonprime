## A 50KB photo that still looks like you

Fifty kilobytes shows up all the time on job portals, exam registrations, student ID systems and HR platforms. It's small enough for them to store thousands of photos cheaply. It's also big enough for a clear portrait, as long as the compression is done with some care.

This page starts with a 50KB target and a maximum width of 1200 pixels. The tool looks for the best quality that fits and only reduces the dimensions when it has to.

## How to compress an image to 50KB

1. **Drop your photo** or tap **Choose files**. On a phone, pick it from your gallery.
2. **Give it a second** until you see **Fits target**.
3. **Download** the new file and upload that one in your form.

Got several photos? Drop up to 20 together and tap **Download all**.

A face against a plain wall keeps the most detail. Busy backgrounds eat into the budget.

## Spend a minute on the photo first

It honestly makes more difference than any setting:

- **Crop to head and shoulders**, unless the form asks for more.
- **Face a window** so the light on your face is soft and even.
- **Stand in front of a plain, light wall.**
- **Use the back camera.** It's sharper than the selfie one.

If the form wants a set shape like 35 x 45 mm, make the photo with the [passport size photo creator](tool:passport-size-photo) first, then compress it here. The 300 DPI setting from the passport photo is kept, so the file still prints at the right size.

## Read the wording on the form

"Maximum 50KB" means 50KB or less. "Between 20KB and 50KB" adds a minimum, so don't go smaller than you need. If the form also gives a size in pixels, set that in the [image resizer](tool:image-resizer) and then compress. There's a full worked example with a photo, signature and ID card in our guide on [how to compress a photo for online application forms](post:compress-photo-for-online-forms).

## Why not just lower the quality?

For small savings, that works fine. But a 12 megapixel photo simply has too many pixels to fit in 50KB at a quality anyone would want. You'd get blocky skin and smeared edges. Bringing the size down to something sensible and then using a moderate quality gives a far nicer picture. Our step-by-step guide on [how to compress an image to 50KB](post:how-to-compress-image-to-50kb) shows the difference.

## The "file too large" trap

Some sites count 1KB as 1,000 bytes and others as 1,024. A file of 51,000 bytes is 49.8KB on one system and 51KB on another. To stay safe, Lumi Pix aims under the stricter number. If it fits here, it fits everywhere.

## If something goes wrong

- **The photo looks soft.** Crop tighter or retake it against a plain wall.
- **The portal says "invalid format".** Upload the JPG you downloaded, not the original HEIC or PNG.
- **It's below the form's minimum.** Raise the maximum width under **Advanced options**.
- **The form keeps picking the original.** On a phone, choose Files or Downloads instead of the gallery.

## Where you'll see a 50KB limit

- **Recruitment and testing portals**, for candidate photos
- **School and university admissions**, for applicant photos and student cards
- **Some visa and travel forms**, usually with fixed dimensions too
- **HR systems and staff directories**

Always check the exact wording. Some give a range like 10 to 50KB, and some add dimensions.

## 50KB vs 100KB

At 50KB a portrait is usually 700 to 1000 pixels wide. At 100KB it can be 1300 to 1600 pixels. On a form that shows the photo at 200 pixels, you can't tell them apart. On an admit card printed at 3 to 4 cm, both look sharp. So if the form says 50KB, don't worry about quality. Just start with a good photo.
