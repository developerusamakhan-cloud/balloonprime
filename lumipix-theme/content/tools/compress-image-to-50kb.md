## A 50KB photo that still looks like you

Fifty kilobytes is a common limit for photographs on application portals, exam registrations, student ID systems and HR platforms. It is small enough to store thousands of photos cheaply, yet large enough for a clear portrait if the compression is done carefully.

This page is set to a 50KB target with a maximum width of 1200 pixels. Drop a photo and the tool finds the best quality that fits, reducing the dimensions only when necessary. The result is a JPG that uploads first time.

## Three steps

1. **Drop your photo** or tap **Choose files**. On a phone, pick it from your gallery.
2. **Wait a moment** until the result shows **Fits target**.
3. **Download** and upload the new file in your form.

To prepare several photos, drop up to 20 together and use **Download all**.

## What to expect

| Original | Typical 50KB result |
|---|---|
| Phone portrait, 4032×3024, about 3–4MB | Around 700–1000 px wide, clear face |
| Scanned passport photo, 1200×1600 | Often full size, very sharp |
| Outdoor photo with lots of detail | Around 600–800 px wide |
| Screenshot of text | Readable, around 900–1200 px wide |

A face against a plain wall keeps the most detail. Busy backgrounds use more of the budget.

## Prepare the photo first

A few seconds of preparation makes a big difference:

- **Crop to head and shoulders** unless the form asks for more.
- **Use soft, even light**, such as a window in front of you.
- **Stand in front of a plain, light wall.**
- **Use the rear camera**, which is sharper than the selfie camera.

If the form specifies proportions such as 35×45 mm, make the photo with the [passport size photo maker](tool:passport-size-photo) first, then compress it here. The compressor keeps the 300 DPI value from the passport photo, so the file remains print-ready.

## Reading the form's requirements

Forms describe limits in different ways. "Maximum 50KB" means 50KB or less. "Between 20KB and 50KB" adds a minimum, so do not compress further than needed. If dimensions in pixels are also given, resize to them with the [Image Resizer](tool:image-resizer) and then compress. For a full example of an application with a photo, a signature and an ID card, see our guide to [compressing photos for online application forms](post:compress-photo-for-online-forms).

## Why not just lower the quality?

Lowering JPG quality alone works for modest savings. But a 12-megapixel photo contains too many pixels to fit in 50KB at a quality anyone would want to look at. The result would be blocky skin and smeared edges. Reducing the dimensions to a sensible size, then using a moderate quality, gives a far better image. Our detailed guide on [how to compress an image to 50KB](post:how-to-compress-image-to-50kb) explains this with examples.

## KB vs KiB: avoiding "file too large"

Some websites count 1KB as 1,000 bytes and others as 1,024. A file of 51,000 bytes is 49.8KB in one system and 51KB in the other. To avoid rejections, Lumi Pix aims under the stricter definition, so a file that fits here fits everywhere.

## Common problems and fixes

- **The photo looks soft.** Crop tighter or retake it against a plain wall, so more detail fits.
- **The portal says invalid format.** Upload the downloaded JPG, not the original HEIC or PNG.
- **The file is too small for a minimum.** Raise the maximum width under **Advanced options**.
- **The upload button picks the original.** On phones, choose Files or Downloads rather than the gallery.

## Where you will meet a 50KB limit

- **Recruitment and testing portals** for candidate photographs.
- **University and school admissions**, for applicant photos and student ID cards.
- **Visa and travel forms** in some countries, alongside fixed dimensions.
- **HR systems and staff directories**, where thousands of photos are stored.
- **Exam registrations**, where the photo is printed on an admit card.

In each case, check the exact wording. Some state a range, such as 10–50KB, and some add dimensions.

## 50KB vs 100KB: what is the difference?

At 50KB, a portrait is typically 700–1000 pixels wide. At 100KB, it can be 1300–1600 pixels wide. On a form that displays the photo at 200 pixels, both look the same. On an admit card printed at 3–4 cm, both are sharp. So if the form says 50KB, there is no need to worry about quality; just prepare a good original.

## Phone-only workflow

1. Ask someone to take your photo with the rear camera against a plain wall.
2. Crop it to head and shoulders in the gallery app.
3. Open this page, tap **Choose files** and pick the cropped photo.
4. Download, then upload from Downloads (Android) or Files (iPhone).

The whole process takes under two minutes and needs no app.

## Checklist

- JPG format
- Under 50KB
- Face clear at 100% zoom
- Plain background
- Compressed copy selected in the form, not the original
