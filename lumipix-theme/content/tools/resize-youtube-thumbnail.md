## Thumbnails that fit YouTube perfectly

The thumbnail is often the first thing viewers judge a video by. YouTube recommends thumbnails of **1280×720 pixels** in a 16:9 shape, with a file size under its upload limit (check YouTube Help for the current limit). This tool resizes any image to exactly 1280×720 and saves a JPG that is usually well under the limit. It runs in your browser, so your designs are never uploaded.

## How to make a thumbnail

1. **Drop your image**: a frame from the video, a photo or a design.
2. The **1280×720** size is already set.
3. **Choose Fill** to crop the image to 16:9, or **Fit** to keep the whole image with a background colour.
4. **Download** the JPG and upload it in YouTube Studio.

If your thumbnail has large bold text and flat colours, you can choose **PNG** under **Save as** for crisper edges; check that the file stays under the limit.

## Why 1280×720?

It is large enough to look sharp on TVs and big screens, and small enough to load quickly in search results and on phones. Images with the same 16:9 shape, such as 1920×1080, also work, but 1280×720 keeps the file smaller. The Full HD preset is here too if you prefer it.

## Designing a thumbnail that gets clicks

- **One clear subject.** A face or a single object reads well at small sizes.
- **Large text, few words.** Three to five words in a bold font.
- **High contrast.** Bright subjects on darker backgrounds stand out in the feed.
- **Consistent style.** Similar colours and layouts help viewers recognise your channel.
- **Avoid small details.** Thumbnails are often shown at around 200 pixels wide.

Removing a busy background helps a subject stand out. Use the [Background Remover](tool:background-remover), choose a solid colour, then resize here.

## Keep important content away from the corners

YouTube overlays the video length in the bottom-right corner, and some layouts show other badges. Keep text and faces out of that corner. When using **Fill**, check that the crop has not cut off any important part of the design.

## Making a thumbnail from a video frame

Pause the video at a clear moment and take a screenshot, or export a frame from your editor. Screenshots are often PNG files with extra borders. Crop the borders, drop the image here, and choose **Fill**. The result is a clean 1280×720 JPG.

## File size tips

Most 1280×720 JPGs are between 100KB and 500KB. If a design with gradients and photos is larger than YouTube allows, compress it with [compress to 2MB](tool:compress-image-to-2mb) or a lower target in the [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image).

## A simple thumbnail workflow

1. Export a clear frame from your video, or design a thumbnail in any editor at roughly 16:9.
2. If the subject should stand out, remove the background and choose a bold colour.
3. Drop the image here, keep 1280×720 and choose **Fill**.
4. Download the JPG and upload it in YouTube Studio when you publish the video.

Keep a folder of past thumbnails. Using a consistent layout and colour scheme makes your videos easy to recognise in recommendations and search results, which can help viewers find your next upload.

## Common thumbnail mistakes

- **Too much text.** Viewers see thumbnails small; three to five words is plenty.
- **Low contrast.** Dark text on a dark photo disappears in the feed.
- **Important details in the bottom-right corner,** where the video length is shown.
- **Wrong shape.** Square or vertical images get bars or crops; always use 16:9.
- **Blurry source.** Start from a sharp frame or a high-resolution design.

## Test it small

Before uploading, view your thumbnail at about 200 pixels wide, roughly the size viewers see on phones. If the text is still readable and the subject still stands out, the thumbnail works. If not, simplify the design, enlarge the text or increase the contrast, then resize again.
