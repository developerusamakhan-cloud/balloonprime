## Thumbnails that fit YouTube

YouTube recommends **1280 x 720 pixels** in a 16:9 shape, with a file under its upload limit. Check YouTube Help for the current limit, because it can change. This tool resizes to exactly 1280 x 720 and saves a JPG that's usually well under it.

## How to make a YouTube thumbnail

1. **Drop your image.** A frame from the video, a photo or a design all work.
2. **1280 x 720** is already set.
3. **Choose Fill** to crop to 16:9, or **Fit** to keep the whole image with a background colour.

## Why 1280 x 720?

It's big enough to look sharp on TVs and large screens, and small enough to load fast in search results and on phones. Other 16:9 sizes like 1920 x 1080 work too, but 1280 x 720 keeps the file smaller. ## What makes people click

- **One clear subject.** A face or a single object reads well when it's small.
- **Big text, few words.** Three to five words in a bold font is plenty.
- **Strong contrast.** A bright subject on a darker background stands out in the feed.
- **A consistent look.** Similar colours and layouts help people spot your channel.
- **No tiny details.** Thumbnails are often shown around 200 pixels wide.

## Keep the corners clear

YouTube puts the video length in the bottom right corner, and some layouts add other badges. Keep text and faces out of there. If you use **Fill**, check the crop hasn't cut off anything important.

## Mistakes that cost clicks

- **Too much text.** People see thumbnails small. Three to five words is enough.
- **Low contrast.** Dark text on a dark photo vanishes in the feed.
- **Important stuff in the bottom right corner**, where the video length sits.
- **The wrong shape.** Square or tall images get bars or crops. Always use 16:9.
- **A blurry starting image.** Begin with a sharp frame or a high resolution design.

## Test it at small size

Before you upload, look at your thumbnail at about 200 pixels wide, which is roughly what people see on their phones. If the text is still readable and the subject still stands out, it works. If not, simplify it, make the text bigger or add contrast, then resize again.
