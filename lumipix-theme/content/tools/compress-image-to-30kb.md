## The middle ground between 20KB and 50KB

A 30KB limit sits in an awkward spot. It is too small for a full phone photo, yet a little more generous than the strictest signature limits. You will find it on application forms that ask for a small photograph and a signature, on membership and booking systems, on forums and chat apps that store avatars, and on websites that keep thumbnails light.

The extra 10KB over a 20KB limit matters more than it sounds. It is half again as much room, which usually means a portrait can be a few hundred pixels wider and a signature keeps smoother pen strokes.

This page opens with a 30KB target and a maximum width of 800 pixels. The quick buttons switch between **20KB**, **30KB**, **40KB** and **50KB**, so you can match the exact limit on your form without typing. If yours is different, enter it in **Custom size** and pick KB or MB.

1. **Crop the image first** so only the face, signature or subject remains.

4. **Wait for the result badge** to confirm the file fits.

## How the compressor reaches 30KB

The tool does not simply crush the quality. It searches for the highest quality setting that still fits under 30KB. Only if even a low quality would be too large does it reduce the dimensions, in small steps, until the file fits. That keeps the picture as sharp as the limit allows instead of turning it into blocky patches.

Because the starting width is capped at 800 pixels, most phone photos are scaled down straight away, which leaves a comfortable quality budget. If you save as JPG and the original was a JPG, its DPI value is kept, so a photo prepared at 300 DPI still reports 300 DPI after compression.

## Tips for a cleaner result

- **Start from the original file.** A screenshot or a photo forwarded through a chat app has already lost detail.
- **Use even, bright light.** Dark photos carry noise, and noise is expensive to compress.
- **Pick a plain background.** A light wall leaves more of the budget for the face.
- **Crop tightly.** Every pixel of empty space costs bytes. Use [Crop Image](tool:crop-image) if your gallery app has no crop tool.
- **Compress once.** If you need a different size, go back to the original rather than compressing the compressed copy.

## Common problems and fixes

- **The result is below the form's minimum.** Open **Advanced options** and raise **Maximum width (px, optional)**, for example to 1000, so more pixels are kept.
- **The portal rejects the format.** Choose **JPG** under **Save as**. Many forms do not accept WebP.
- **The pixel dimensions are wrong.** Some forms want, say, 200×230 pixels. Resize to that with the [Image Resizer](tool:image-resizer) first, then compress here.
- **The photo looks soft.** Crop closer and retake against a plain wall in daylight, or use the **40KB** preset if the form allows it.
- **The upload picks the original.** On phones, select the file from Downloads or Files instead of the photo gallery.
- **It says just over the limit.** Some sites count 1KB as 1,024 bytes. Lumi Pix aims under the stricter figure, so make sure you uploaded the downloaded copy.

## Why a profile picture fits so well at 30KB

Avatars are usually shown in a circle or square between 40 and 200 pixels across. Even on high-density screens, a 400 pixel image is more than enough. At 30KB that size leaves plenty of quality, so the picture looks crisp in comments, chats and staff directories while the site stays fast for everyone.

## Thumbnails for websites and listings

If you run a small website or a listing page, thumbnails are often the most numerous images on the page. Keeping each one near 30KB means a grid of twenty previews loads quickly even on a slow mobile connection. Set the maximum width to roughly twice the width the thumbnail is displayed at, choose **WebP** under **Save as** if your site supports it, and compress the whole set in one batch.
