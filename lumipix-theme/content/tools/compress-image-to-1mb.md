## Under 1MB without visible loss

A 1MB limit is common on websites, marketplaces, email services, ticketing systems and support forms. Modern phone and camera photos easily exceed it: a 12-megapixel phone photo is often 3–5MB, and camera files can be 10MB or more. The good news is that at 1MB you can keep almost all of the visible quality.

This page is set to 1MB. Drop your photos and the compressor keeps the full resolution where possible, lowering the quality only as much as needed. For most photos, you will not be able to tell the result from the original at normal viewing size.

## How to compress to 1MB

1. **Drop your photos** or click **Choose files**. You can add up to 20 at once.
2. **Wait for the Fits target badge** on each result.
3. **Download** one at a time or use **Download all**.

Want a different limit, such as 2MB or 500KB? Change the number and unit in the **Custom size** field; the images are compressed again automatically.

## What to expect

| Original | Original size | Result under 1MB |
|---|---|---|
| Phone photo 4032×3024 | 3–5MB | Usually full size, quality around 80–90% |
| Camera photo 6000×4000 | 10–15MB | Usually full size, slightly lower quality |
| Panorama 12000×3000 | 15MB+ | Slightly reduced dimensions |
| PNG screenshot | 1–3MB | Converted to JPG, full size |

The compressor reduces dimensions only when quality alone cannot reach the target, which is rare at 1MB.

## Good uses for 1MB

- **Email attachments**, so messages send and open quickly without losing quality.
- **Marketplace and classified listings**, which often cap images at 1MB or similar.
- **Support tickets and forms** that accept screenshots and photos.
- **Sharing photos** with family without filling everyone's storage.
- **Blog posts**, although for websites 200–300KB per image is usually better for speed.

## Email: a practical example

Imagine sending ten holiday photos of about 4MB each. That is a 40MB email, which many services will refuse. Compressing each photo to under 1MB brings the total under 10MB, and on a phone or laptop screen the photos look the same. Drop all ten here, click **Download all**, and attach the compressed copies.

## Keep the originals for printing

Compression is permanent. A 1MB copy is perfect for screens, but for large prints you want the full original. Always keep originals in a separate folder and compress copies for sharing. If you need print-ready files with a specific size and DPI, use the [Image Resizer](tool:image-resizer) with centimetres or inches instead.

## JPG or WebP?

JPG is the safe choice for email and uploads. If you are preparing images for your own website, choose **WebP** under **Save as**: it is typically smaller at the same visual quality. Make sure the website or app you are uploading to accepts WebP before you switch.

## Tips for the best quality

- **Do not re-compress** files that have already been compressed; start from the original.
- **Avoid PNG for photos.** PNG photos are huge; the compressor converts them to efficient JPGs.
- **Crop away what you do not need** before compressing, especially for panoramas.

For a deeper guide to making images smaller while keeping them sharp, read [how to lower image file size without losing quality](post:how-to-lower-image-file-size), and if you are unsure which format to use, see [PNG vs JPG](post:png-vs-jpg).

## Private and unlimited

Photos are processed on your own device and never uploaded. There is no account, watermark or daily limit, so you can compress an entire album if you need to.

## Works on phones and computers

Use it in any modern browser on Android, iPhone, Windows or Mac. iPhone HEIC photos are converted to JPG automatically. On phones, compressed files are saved in Downloads (Android) or the Files app (iPhone).

## Common 1MB situations

**Classified ads and marketplaces.** Listings often limit photo uploads. Compressing each photo to 1MB keeps them sharp and uploads them quickly, even on mobile data.

**Customer support and warranty claims.** Help desks frequently accept attachments up to around 1–2MB. A clear photo of a receipt or a damaged item under 1MB is accepted first time.

**School and work portals.** Assignment and HR systems may cap attachments at 1MB. Photos of handwritten work, whiteboards and documents stay readable at this size.

**Sharing with family.** Large albums fill phones and cloud storage quickly. 1MB copies look the same on phone screens and take a fraction of the space.

## 1MB vs 500KB vs 2MB

| Target | Typical result for a 12-megapixel photo | When to choose it |
|---|---|---|
| 500KB | Full or slightly reduced size, good quality | Fast email, messaging |
| 1MB | Full size, very good quality | Most uploads and sharing |
| 2MB | Full size, near-original quality | Archiving copies, high-quality sharing |

Type the number you need in the **Custom size** field and choose KB or MB.

## Batch example

Select all the photos from an event, up to 20 at a time, drop them here and click **Download all**. The compressed copies keep their original names with "-compressed" added, so you can tell them apart. Upload or attach the copies, and keep the originals somewhere safe for printing.

## A note on screenshots

Screenshots are often saved as PNG and can be several megabytes. Converting them to JPG under 1MB works well for photos and app screens. For screenshots full of small text, check readability at 100% zoom; if the text looks soft, keep the PNG or use a higher limit.
