## Getting cleaner edges

- **Contrast helps.** When the subject stands out from the background, the model has clear edges to follow.
- **Keep the subject sharp.** Blurry edges are hard to separate.
- **Use a big original.** More pixels means more edge detail.
- **Fill the frame.** If the subject is tiny in a wide scene, crop closer first.

A few rough hairs? Putting the cut-out on a colour close to the original background usually hides them.

## PNG or JPG?

Pick **PNG** when you need transparency, for example to drop the subject into a design. Pick **JPG** when you've chosen a solid background, like white for a listing or a form, because the file is much smaller. JPG can't store transparency, so if you choose JPG with the transparent option, the background turns white.

## Tips for cut-outs that look professional

- **Shoot against a plain wall** if you know you'll remove the background later. It gives the cleanest edges.
- **Leave space around the subject** so hair and shoulders aren't cut off by the frame.
- **Match the light.** A warmly lit cut-out looks odd on a cold blue background.
- **Add a soft shadow** in a design app if the subject looks like it's floating.

## Where AI still struggles

AI background removal is impressive, but it isn't perfect. Glass and other see-through objects, fine hair against a similar colour, and subjects that blend into the scene can all be tricky. For those, editing by hand in a desktop editor gives you the most control.
