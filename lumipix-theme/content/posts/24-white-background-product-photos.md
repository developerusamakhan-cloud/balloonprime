White background product photos make a shop look trustworthy and tidy, and lots of marketplaces insist on a white or plain background for the main image. You don't need a studio for it. A phone, decent light and a free background remover will do the job.

## Step 1: Take a good photo of the product

The AI can replace the background, but it can't fix a blurry or badly lit photo.

- **Use soft daylight.** Put the product near a window, out of direct sun.
- **Use a plain backdrop.** A sheet of white paper or card curved up behind the product makes the cut-out much easier.
- **Fill the frame.** Get close, but leave a little space around the product.
- **Keep the camera steady**, and at the product's height for a natural angle.
- **Take several angles:** front, side, back and close-ups.

## Step 2: Remove the background

1. Open the tool to [remove background for free online](tool:background-remover).
2. Drop a product photo in.
3. Choose the **white** swatch as the new background.
4. Download as **JPG** for a white background, or **PNG** for a transparent one to use in designs.

The AI runs on your device, so even products you haven't launched yet stay private. The full resolution file is free. There's also a page set up to [change photo background to white](tool:white-background-photo) with white already selected.

## Step 3: Make the catalogue consistent

Shoppers notice when every product is a different size and sits in a different spot. For a consistent look:

- Use the [image resizer](tool:image-resizer) to give every product the same square size, like 1080 x 1080 or 2000 x 2000.
- Choose **Fit** with white, so the whole product shows with even space around it.
- Use the same camera angle for similar products.

## Step 4: Make them light enough for the web

Big images slow your shop down. After resizing, use the [image compressor](tool:compress-image) to get each photo to around 150 to 300KB. Our guide on [how to lower image file size](post:how-to-lower-image-file-size) explains how to do that without any visible loss.

## Marketplace rules

Many marketplaces have rules for the main product image, like a pure white background, a minimum size and the product filling most of the frame. The rules vary between platforms and change over time, so check the seller guidelines of the marketplace you use.

## A simple home studio

You don't need special kit, but a small setup makes every photo match:

| Item | What it's for | Cheap option |
|---|---|---|
| White sweep | A smooth background with no visible edge | A big sheet of white card curved against a wall |
| Soft light | Even light without harsh shadows | A window with a thin white curtain |
| Reflector | Fills in shadows on the dark side | White foam board or card |
| Something to steady the phone | Sharp, matching angles | A stack of books or a small tripod |

Put the product in the middle of the sweep, the window on one side and the reflector on the other. Shoot every product in the same spot.

## Angles that sell

- **The front** as the main image.
- **A 45 degree angle** to show depth.
- **The side and back** for practical details.
- **Close-ups** of textures, labels and buttons.
- **A scale shot** with a hand or an everyday object, so buyers can tell how big it is.

Keep the camera at the same height for every product in a category, so the catalogue looks uniform.

## Tricky products

- **Shiny products.** Use soft, spread-out light to avoid bright hot spots.
- **Clear glass.** AI can struggle here. Shoot it against white so it already looks clean.
- **White products.** Shoot against light grey for contrast, then swap the background for white.

For flat items like printed cards and labels on white paper, it's quicker to [remove white background from image](tool:remove-white-background) files with the colour tool.

## Doing a whole catalogue

Once the photos are taken:

1. Remove the background from each one and choose white.
2. Drop them all into the resizer, or [resize images in bulk](tool:resize-image-in-bulk), set a square size like 2000 x 2000, and choose **Fit** with white.
3. Click **Download all**.
4. Compress the set for the web at around 200 to 300KB each.

You've now got a matching set of images ready for a shop or marketplace.

## Shadows and realism

A product floating on pure white can look a bit flat. Plenty of shops add a soft shadow underneath to ground it. You can:

- Keep the natural shadow by photographing on white and using the white background remover with a low tolerance, so the soft grey shadow stays.
- Add a gentle shadow in a design tool after removing the background.

Whichever you choose, do it for every product so they match.

## File names and alt text for SEO

Search engines read image file names and alt text. Before you upload:

- Rename files so they describe the product, like `blue-ceramic-mug-front.jpg` instead of `IMG_4831.jpg`.
- Write alt text that describes the product, like "Blue ceramic mug with handle, front view".
- Keep images reasonably small so product pages load fast on phones.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixed lighting**, like daylight plus a yellow lamp, gives colour casts that are hard to fix.
- **Messy edges**, like a table edge showing behind the product, make the cut-out harder.
- **Different sizes** in a category grid look untidy. Use the same canvas for all of them.
- **Over-compressed images** make products look cheap. Check them at 100% zoom.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I get a white background on product photos for free?

Photograph the product in soft light, run it through an AI background remover, choose white as the new background and download the full-size image.

### What size should product photos be?

Use the same square size for all of them, like 1080 x 1080 or 2000 x 2000 pixels, and check your marketplace's minimum requirements.

### Should I use PNG or JPG for product photos?

JPG with a white background for listings, because the files are smaller. PNG when you need transparency for designs.

### What background colour is best for product photos?

Pure white is the most common choice and is required for main images on many marketplaces. Light grey works well for extra images too.

### How do I keep product photos consistent?

Shoot every product in the same spot with the same light and camera height, then resize them all to the same square canvas with white around them.

### Do I need a professional camera for product photos?

No. A recent phone, soft window light and a white backdrop give great results for most online shops, especially once the background is cleaned up.
