Clean product photos on a white background make a shop look trustworthy and consistent, and many marketplaces require a white or plain background for the main image. You do not need a studio. A phone, good light and a free background remover will do.

## Step 1: Shoot the product well

The AI can replace the background, but it cannot fix a blurry or badly lit photo.

- **Use soft daylight.** Place the product near a window, out of direct sun.
- **Use a plain backdrop.** A sheet of white paper or card curved up behind the product makes cutting out easier.
- **Fill the frame.** Get close, but leave a small margin around the product.
- **Keep the camera steady** and at the product's height for a natural angle.
- **Shoot several angles:** front, side, back and details.

## Step 2: Remove the background

1. Open the [Background Remover](tool:background-remover).
2. Drop a product photo.
3. Choose the **white** swatch as the new background.
4. Download as **JPG** (white background) or **PNG** (transparent, for designs).

The AI runs on your device, so even unreleased products stay private. The full-resolution file is free to download.

## Step 3: Make your catalogue consistent

Shoppers notice when every product is a different size and position. For a consistent look:

- Use the [Image Resizer](tool:image-resizer) with the same square size for every product, for example 1080 × 1080 or 2000 × 2000.
- Choose **Fit – add background** with white, so the whole product shows with even padding.
- Keep the same camera angle across similar products.

## Step 4: Optimise for the web

Large images slow your store down. After resizing, use the [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image) to get each photo to around 150–300KB. Our guide to [lowering image file size](post:how-to-lower-image-file-size) explains how to do this without visible quality loss.

## Marketplace requirements

Many marketplaces have rules for the main product image, such as a pure white background, a minimum size and the product filling most of the frame. Rules differ between platforms and change over time, so check the seller guidelines of the marketplace you use.

## Tips for tricky products

- **Shiny products:** use diffused light to avoid hot spots.
- **Transparent glass:** AI may struggle. Shoot against a white background so it already looks clean.
- **White products:** use a light grey backdrop for contrast during the shoot, then replace it with white.

For flat items such as printed cards and labels on white paper, the [white background remover](tool:remove-white-background) is faster. To compare all the options, see our [background remover comparison](post:remove-bg-alternatives).

## A simple home studio for product photos

You do not need special equipment, but a small setup makes every photo consistent:

| Item | Purpose | Budget option |
|---|---|---|
| White sweep | Seamless background | A large sheet of white card curved against a wall |
| Soft light | Even lighting without harsh shadows | A window with a thin white curtain |
| Reflector | Fills shadows on the dark side | White foam board or card |
| Stable phone | Sharp, consistent angles | A stack of books or a small tripod |

Place the product in the middle of the sweep, the window to one side, and the reflector on the other side. Shoot every product in the same spot.

## Shooting angles that sell

- **Front view** as the main image.
- **45-degree angle** to show depth.
- **Side and back** for practical details.
- **Close-ups** of textures, labels and buttons.
- **Scale shot** with a hand or a common object, so buyers understand the size.

Keep the camera height the same for every product in a category, so the catalogue looks uniform.

## Batch editing a whole catalogue

Once the photos are taken:

1. Remove the background of each photo with the [Background Remover](tool:background-remover), choosing white.
2. Drop all results into the [Image Resizer](tool:image-resizer), set a square size such as 2000 × 2000, and choose **Fit – add background** with white.
3. Click **Download all**.
4. Compress the set for the web with the [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image) at around 200–300KB each.

You now have a consistent set of images ready for a shop or marketplace.

## Shadows and realism

A product floating on pure white can look flat. Many shops add a soft shadow underneath to ground it. Options:

- Keep the natural shadow by photographing on white and using the [white background remover](tool:remove-white-background) with a low tolerance, so the soft grey shadow stays.
- Add a gentle shadow in a design tool after removing the background.

Whatever you choose, apply it to every product for consistency.

## File names and alt text for SEO

Search engines read image file names and alt text. Before uploading:

- Rename files descriptively, for example `blue-ceramic-mug-front.jpg` instead of `IMG_4831.jpg`.
- Write alt text that describes the product, such as "Blue ceramic mug with handle, front view".
- Keep images reasonably small so product pages load quickly on mobile.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixed lighting**, such as daylight plus a yellow lamp, gives colour casts that are hard to fix.
- **Cluttered edges**, like table edges visible behind the product, make the cut-out harder.
- **Inconsistent sizes** in a category grid look messy; use the same canvas for all.
- **Over-compressed images** make products look cheap; check at 100% zoom.

## Key takeaways

- **Soft daylight and a white sweep** give the best starting photos.
- **Remove the background and choose white**, then place every product on the same square canvas.
- **Batch process** resizing and compression to keep the catalogue consistent.
- **Name files and write alt text** that describe each product.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I get a white background on product photos for free?

Photograph the product in soft light, use an AI background remover, choose white as the new background and download the full-size image.

### What size should product photos be?

Use a consistent square size such as 1080 × 1080 or 2000 × 2000 pixels, and check your marketplace's minimum requirements.

### Should I use PNG or JPG for product photos?

JPG with a white background for listings, because the files are smaller. PNG when you need transparency for designs.

### What background colour is best for product photos?

Pure white is the most common choice and is required for main images on many marketplaces. Light grey also works well for secondary images.

### How do I keep product photos consistent?

Shoot every product in the same spot with the same light and camera height, then resize all images to the same square canvas with white padding.

### Do I need a professional camera for product photos?

No. A recent phone camera, soft window light and a white backdrop produce excellent results for most online shops, especially once the background is cleaned up.
