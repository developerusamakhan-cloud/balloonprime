Whether an email bounces, a website refuses an upload or your phone storage is full, the fix is the same: make the image file smaller. This guide explains what actually controls file size and how to reduce it on any device.

## What decides an image's file size?

Three things matter, in this order:

1. **Pixel dimensions.** A 4000×3000 photo has 12 million pixels. A 2000×1500 version has 3 million, a quarter as many. Halving the width and height roughly quarters the file size.
2. **Compression quality.** JPG and WebP throw away detail you are unlikely to notice. Lower quality means smaller files, up to the point where blocks and smudges appear.
3. **Format.** Photos saved as PNG are often five to ten times larger than the same photo as JPG. Graphics with flat colours are the exception, where PNG can be smaller.

The content of the picture matters too: a plain blue sky compresses much better than gravel or leaves.

## The fastest method on any device

Use a compressor that lets you choose the size in KB. Open the [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image), type the size you need, for example 100, and drop your image. The tool adjusts quality and, if necessary, dimensions until the file fits. It works the same on Android, iPhone, Windows and Mac because it runs in the browser, and your photos are never uploaded.

If you know the target, the preset pages are even quicker: [50KB](tool:compress-image-to-50kb), [100KB](tool:compress-image-to-100kb), [200KB](tool:compress-image-to-200kb) or [1MB](tool:compress-image-to-1mb).

## Built-in options on each device

**Windows:** open the image in Paint, choose **Resize**, reduce the percentage, then save as JPEG. The Photos app also has a **Resize image** option in its menu. Neither lets you pick an exact KB size, so you may need a few attempts.

**Mac:** open the image in Preview, choose **Tools → Adjust Size** to reduce the dimensions, then **File → Export**, choose JPEG and move the quality slider. Preview shows the resulting file size as you drag.

**iPhone:** the Photos app does not offer a KB option. A browser-based compressor is the simplest route. To make future photos smaller and more compatible, go to Settings → Camera → Formats and choose **Most Compatible**, which saves JPG instead of HEIC.

**Android:** some gallery apps include a resize option. Otherwise use a browser-based tool, which avoids installing an app that may want access to all your photos.

## How small can you go?

It depends on what the image is for:

| Use | Typical size |
|---|---|
| Signature for a form | 10–30KB |
| Passport-style photo for a form | 20–100KB |
| Document scan | 100–300KB |
| Email attachment | under 1MB |
| Website image | 100–300KB |

For forms, the requirement is set by the organisation. Our guide to [compressing photos for online application forms](post:compress-photo-for-online-forms) walks through the full process.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Compressing the same JPG again and again.** Each save loses more detail. Always start from the original.
- **Converting a photo to PNG to "improve quality".** It only makes the file bigger.
- **Shrinking a tiny image to save space.** If it is already under the limit, leave it alone.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I reduce a photo from MB to KB?

Use a compressor with a target size. Enter the KB limit, drop the photo, and the tool reduces quality and dimensions until it fits.

### Does reducing image size reduce quality?

A little detail is always lost, but at sensible sizes the difference is hard to see on screen. Reducing dimensions and quality together gives the best-looking result.

### Which format gives the smallest file?

For photos, WebP and JPG are the most efficient. JPG is accepted almost everywhere, so it is the safest choice for forms.
