An email bounces, a website refuses your upload, or your phone tells you storage is full. Different problems, same fix: make the image file smaller. This guide explains what actually decides file size, and how to reduce image size in KB on Android, iPhone, Windows and Mac.

## What decides how big an image file is?

Three things, in this order:

1. **Pixel dimensions.** A 4000 x 3000 photo has 12 million pixels. A 2000 x 1500 version has 3 million, a quarter as many. Halve the width and height and the file usually drops to about a quarter.
2. **Compression quality.** JPG and WebP throw away detail you're unlikely to notice. Lower quality means a smaller file, up to the point where blocks and smudges start showing.
3. **Format.** Photos saved as PNG are often five to ten times bigger than the same photo as JPG. Graphics with flat colours are the exception, where PNG can be smaller.

What's in the picture matters too. A plain blue sky compresses much better than gravel or leaves.

## What's already on your device

**Windows.** Open the image in Paint, choose **Resize**, lower the percentage and save as JPEG. The Photos app also has a **Resize image** option in its menu. Neither lets you pick an exact KB size, so it can take a few goes.

**Mac.** Open the image in Preview, choose **Tools**, then **Adjust Size** to make it smaller, then **File**, **Export**. Pick JPEG and move the quality slider. Preview shows the file size as you drag, which helps.

**iPhone.** The Photos app has no KB option, so a browser-based compressor is the simplest route. To make future photos smaller and easier to share, go to Settings, Camera, Formats and choose **Most Compatible**. That saves JPG instead of HEIC.

**Android.** Some gallery apps have a resize option. Otherwise use a browser tool, which saves you installing an app that wants access to all your photos.

## Making it smaller without changing the dimensions

Sometimes the pixel size has to stay put, for example when a form says 600 x 800 pixels and a maximum of 100KB. Then only the compression quality can change.

1. Set the exact dimensions first in the [image resizer](tool:image-resizer).
2. Open the image compressor, set the KB limit and leave **Maximum width** empty under Advanced options.
3. Now only the quality is lowered. If it still can't fit, the tool tells you, and you'll know the limit is unrealistic for that size and content.

## Different jobs, different targets

**Email.** Most email services take attachments up to around 20 to 25MB in total, but big photos are slow to send and open. Bring each photo under 1MB and they still look sharp on screen.

**Websites.** Aim for 100 to 300KB per large image. Resize to the width it's actually shown at, then compress.

**Forms.** Follow the stated limit exactly.

**Storage.** If your phone is full, compressing old photos frees up space. Keep the originals of anything you might print, though, because compression is permanent.

## KB and MB, explained

File sizes are measured in bytes. A kilobyte (KB) is 1,000 or 1,024 bytes, depending on who's counting, and a megabyte (MB) is about 1,000KB. That tiny difference matters right at the edge of a limit. A file of 102,000 bytes is "99.6KB" on one system and "102KB" on another. Lumi Pix aims under the stricter number, so your file passes either check.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Compressing the same JPG over and over.** Each save loses more detail. Always go back to the original.
- **Converting a photo to PNG to "improve quality".** It just makes the file bigger.
- **Shrinking an image that's already small enough.** If it's under the limit, leave it alone.

## Quick fixes

- **It didn't get any smaller.** It may already be heavily compressed. Try reducing the dimensions instead.
- **It looks blocky.** The quality had to go very low. Make it a little smaller in pixels so a higher quality fits.
- **The size changed after uploading.** Some websites recompress images after you upload them. That's normal and out of your hands.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I reduce a photo from MB to KB?

Use a compressor with a target size. Type the KB limit, drop the photo, and the tool lowers the quality and dimensions until it fits.

### Does reducing image size reduce quality?

A little detail is always lost, but at sensible sizes you'll struggle to see it on screen. Reducing dimensions and quality together gives the best looking result.

### Which format gives the smallest file?

For photos, WebP and JPG are the most efficient. JPG is accepted almost everywhere, so it's the safest choice for forms.

### Can I reduce image size without changing the dimensions?

Yes, by lowering only the compression quality. Leave the maximum width empty in the compressor and set your KB target.

### How many KB is 1MB?

About 1,000KB. Some systems count 1,024KB per MB, which is why sizes can look slightly different on different devices.
