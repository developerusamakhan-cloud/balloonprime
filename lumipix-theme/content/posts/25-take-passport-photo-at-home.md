A studio passport photo costs money and a trip. With a phone, a plain wall and a few minutes, you can take one at home. Whether it is accepted depends on the rules of the authority you apply to, so read their guidance first, then use these steps to meet it.

## What you need

- A phone camera (the rear camera gives better quality than the selfie camera)
- A plain white or light-coloured wall
- A window with daylight, or two lamps
- A helper, or a tripod and timer

## Step 1: Set up the background

Stand about half a metre to a metre in front of the wall. Standing a little away from it reduces shadows behind your head. Remove posters and anything else from the frame.

## Step 2: Get the lighting right

Face a window so light falls evenly on your face. Avoid overhead lights that cast shadows under your eyes, and direct sunlight that makes you squint. If one side of your face is darker, ask your helper to hold a white sheet of paper or card on that side to bounce light back.

## Step 3: Position the camera

- Hold the camera at **eye level**, about 1–1.5 metres away.
- Do not use wide-angle or portrait (blur) modes.
- Leave space above your head and around your shoulders. You will crop later.

## Step 4: Pose as the rules require

Most authorities ask for a neutral expression, mouth closed, eyes open and looking at the camera, head straight and hair away from the eyes. Remove glasses if the rules require it. Wear clothing that contrasts with the background.

Take several photos and pick the sharpest.

## Step 5: Crop to the exact size

1. Open the [passport photo maker](tool:passport-size-photo).
2. Drop the photo and choose **Passport 35×45 mm**, **US passport 2×2 in** or another size.
3. The tool crops to the right shape at **300 DPI**.
4. Download the JPG.

The size details are explained in our [passport photo size guide](post:passport-photo-size-guide). If your wall was not plain enough, use the [Background Remover](tool:background-remover) with a white background first.

## Step 6: Print or upload

**Printing:** order a standard 4×6 inch (10×15 cm) print and cut it carefully, or arrange several photos on one sheet with a photo editor. The DPI stored in the file tells the print software the correct size.

**Online forms:** many forms also have a file size limit. Use the [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image) to meet it; it keeps the 300 DPI value of your photo. See [how to compress a photo for online application forms](post:compress-photo-for-online-forms).

## Common reasons home photos are rejected

- Shadows on the face or on the background
- Head tilted or not centred
- Photo too small, blurry or over-processed with filters
- Background not plain or not the required colour
- Wrong size or proportions

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I take a passport photo with my phone?

In many countries, yes, if it meets the official requirements for size, background, lighting and pose. Check the issuing authority's guidance.

### What is the best background for a passport photo?

A plain white or off-white background is the most common requirement. Some countries specify a different colour.

### How do I make my photo 35×45 mm?

Use a passport photo maker that crops to 35×45 mm at 300 DPI, which is 413×531 pixels.
