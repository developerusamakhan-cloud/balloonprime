A studio passport photo costs money and a trip out. With a phone, a plain wall and a few minutes, you can take a passport photo at home instead. Whether it gets accepted depends on the rules of the office you're applying to, so read their guidance first, then use these steps to meet it.

## What you need

- A phone camera (the back camera is better than the selfie one)
- A plain white or light-coloured wall
- A window with daylight, or two lamps
- Someone to help, or a tripod and the timer

## Step 1: Set up the background

Stand about half a metre to a metre in front of the wall. Standing a little away from it cuts down shadows behind your head. Take down posters and move anything else out of the frame.

## Step 2: Get the light right

Face a window so the light falls evenly on your face. Avoid ceiling lights, which cast shadows under your eyes, and direct sun, which makes you squint. If one side of your face is darker, ask your helper to hold a sheet of white paper or card on that side to bounce some light back.

## Step 3: Position the camera

- Hold it at **eye level**, about 1 to 1.5 metres away.
- Don't use wide-angle or portrait (background blur) modes.
- Leave space above your head and around your shoulders. You'll crop it later.

## Step 4: Pose the way the rules ask

Most authorities want a neutral expression, mouth closed, eyes open and looking at the camera, head straight and hair away from your eyes. Take your glasses off if the rules say so. Wear something that stands out from the background.

Take several photos and pick the sharpest.

## Step 5: Crop it to the exact size

1. Open the [passport size photo creator](tool:passport-size-photo).
2. Drop the photo in and choose **Passport 35 x 45 mm**, **US passport 2 x 2 in** or another size.
3. The tool crops it to the right shape at **300 DPI**.
4. Download the JPG.

## Step 6: Print or upload

**Printing.** In the passport photo creator, choose a **10 x 15 cm** or **4 x 6 in** print sheet. It fits six 35 x 45 mm copies on one sheet with thin cut lines. Order it as a normal photo print and cut along the lines. The DPI saved in the file tells the print software the right size.

**Online forms.** Lots of forms have a file size limit as well. Use the [image compressor](tool:compress-image) to meet it. It keeps the 300 DPI value of your photo. See [how to compress a photo for online application forms](post:compress-photo-for-online-forms).

## Quick checklist before you start

- A plain, light background with nothing behind your head.
- Soft, even light on your face, ideally from a window in front of you.
- Camera at eye level, about 1 to 1.5 metres away.
- A neutral face, mouth closed, eyes open.
- Hair away from your face, and glasses off if the rules say so.
- Clothes that stand out from the background (no white on white).
- Several shots, so you can pick the sharpest.

## Fixing problems afterwards

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| The background isn't plain | [Remove background for free online](tool:background-remover) and choose white |
| Your face is too small in the frame | Crop closer before choosing the passport size |
| A slight tilt | Rotate it in your phone's editor before cropping |
| Too dark | Turn up the brightness a little, but skip the heavy filters |
| Shadows behind your head | Retake it standing further from the wall |

Avoid beauty filters and heavy retouching. Many authorities reject photos that have been altered.

## Photos of babies and children

Children's passport photos usually have more relaxed rules about expression and eyes, but the background and lighting rules still apply. Lay a baby on a plain white sheet and take the photo from above, or have a child sit in front of a light wall. Take lots of photos quickly and pick the best one. Check the specific rules for children with the issuing office.

## Online applications and photo codes

Some countries' online passport services let you upload a photo you took yourself, while others want a photo from an approved shop that gives you a digital code. If yours accepts your own photos, follow its upload guidance for dimensions and file size.

## Printing at home or at a shop

At home, use glossy or matte photo paper and a decent printer. Set the print size to 100%, not "fit to page", so the photo comes out at exactly 35 x 45 mm or 2 x 2 inches. At a shop, a standard 4 x 6 inch print with several copies on it is the cheapest option.

## Why home photos get rejected

The usual reasons are shadows, an uneven background, a head that's too small or too big, a tilted head, glare on glasses and heavy editing. A little more care with the light and your position fixes most of them. Need a clean background for other things too? See [how to remove the background from an image](post:how-to-remove-background-from-image).

## Keep it for later

Save the final passport photo and the original in a folder. Visas, ID cards, exam registrations and job portals all ask for something similar. With the original saved, you can quickly resize or compress it for each new form.

## The short version

- **A plain light wall, soft light and the camera at eye level** are the three essentials.
- **Crop and size it** with a passport photo tool at 300 DPI.
- **Print at 100% scale**, never "fit to page".

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I take a passport photo with my phone?

In many countries, yes, if it meets the official rules for size, background, lighting and pose. Check the issuing authority's guidance.

### What is the best background for a passport photo?

A plain white or off-white background is the most common requirement. Some countries ask for a different colour.

### How do I make my photo 35x45 mm?

Use a passport photo tool that crops to 35 x 45 mm at 300 DPI, which is 413 x 531 pixels.

### Can I smile in a passport photo?

Most authorities want a neutral expression with your mouth closed. Check the rules of your issuing office.

### How should I print a passport photo at home?

Print at 100% scale on photo paper so it comes out at the exact size, like 35 x 45 mm. Don't use "fit to page".
