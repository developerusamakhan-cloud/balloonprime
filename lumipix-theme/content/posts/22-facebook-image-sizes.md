Facebook shows images in lots of places, and each one has its own shape. Upload at a sensible size and you avoid awkward crops and blurry text. Here are the Facebook image sizes most people need. One thing to bear in mind: Meta changes its layouts now and then, so check its help pages before a big campaign.

## Facebook image sizes at a glance

| Placement | Recommended size | Aspect ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Feed post, square | 1080 x 1080 px | 1:1 |
| Feed post, portrait | 1080 x 1350 px | 4:5 |
| Link preview (shared link image) | 1200 x 630 px | 1.91:1 |
| Story | 1080 x 1920 px | 9:16 |
| Cover photo (page or profile) | 851 x 315 px or bigger | about 2.7:1 |
| Profile picture | at least 320 x 320 px | 1:1, shown as a circle |
| Event cover | 1920 x 1005 px | about 1.91:1 |

## Feed posts

On phones, where most people scroll, square and 4:5 portrait images take up the most room. They're the same sizes Instagram uses, so one image can work on both. Our [Instagram image sizes guide](post:instagram-image-sizes) has more on that.

## Link previews

When you share a link, Facebook uses that page's Open Graph image, ideally 1200 x 630 pixels. Keep important text near the middle, because some placements crop the edges. Every Lumi Pix page and article has a share image at that size for exactly this reason.

## Cover photos

Covers are cropped differently on a computer and on a phone. Upload something bigger than the minimum, like 1640 x 624 for extra sharpness, and keep faces and text in the middle so nothing important gets cut on either. The tool to [resize photo for Facebook cover](tool:resize-image-for-facebook) images has that size ready to go.

## Profile pictures

Profile pictures are shown as a circle. Put your face in the middle, leave a bit of space around it, and upload a square image so you're the one deciding the crop.

## Ads and boosted posts

Ads show up in lots of placements: the feed, stories, the right column and the Audience Network. Rather than designing for each, most advertisers make two versions:

| Version | Size | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Square or 4:5 | 1080 x 1080 or 1080 x 1350 | Feed placements |
| Vertical 9:16 | 1080 x 1920 | Stories and reels |

Keep the text short and away from the edges, and check Meta's current ad guidelines before you launch, because the recommended specs change.

## Group and event covers

Group covers use a wide format, often quoted as 1640 x 856 pixels. As with page covers, phones and computers crop the edges differently, so keep the title and faces in the middle.

Event covers show up in listings, invitations and on the event page. Use a wide image around 1920 x 1005 pixels, keep the text big, and only put the date in the image if the event definitely won't move.

## How Facebook compresses images

Facebook recompresses everything you upload. To keep the quality as high as you can:

- Upload at the recommended size, not giant camera originals.
- Use **sRGB** colour. Most phones and browser tools do this for you.
- Use PNG for images with text or logos, and JPG for photos.
- Don't upload an image that's already been through several apps.

If images still look soft, export at a slightly higher JPG quality, or switch to PNG for graphics.

## A simple routine for page managers

1. Make one master image for each post at a generous size.
2. Make the square, portrait and story versions from it. Turn the link off, type the size and choose **Fill** or **Fit**.
3. For text-heavy graphics, choose **PNG** under **Save as**.
4. Keep the versions together so they're easy to reuse. You can [resize photo for Instagram](tool:resize-image-for-instagram) with the same feed sizes.

## A profile picture that works everywhere

Your profile picture appears in posts, comments, Messenger and search. Use a square image of at least 320 x 320 pixels, centre your face or logo, and leave space around it, because the circle cuts off the corners. For a clean background, [remove background for free online](tool:background-remover) and swap in a solid colour first.

## Fixing awkward crops on shared links

When you share a link, Facebook pulls the page's share image. If it looks cropped or out of date:

- Check the page has an Open Graph image at 1200 x 630.
- Use Meta's Sharing Debugger to refresh the preview after you change it.

If you're making link preview images for your own site, our guide on [how to resize a picture in pixels](post:resize-picture-in-pixels) shows how to hit an exact size.

## The short version

- **Feed posts:** 1080 x 1080 square or 1080 x 1350 portrait.
- **Link previews:** 1200 x 630, with the important bits in the middle.
- **Stories:** 1080 x 1920, with text away from the top and bottom.
- **Covers:** upload bigger than the minimum and keep faces and titles centred.
- **Use PNG for graphics with text**, and JPG for photos.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size should a Facebook post image be?

1080 x 1080 pixels for square posts, or 1080 x 1350 for portrait posts, both work well in the feed.

### What is the best size for a Facebook link preview image?

1200 x 630 pixels, with the important content near the middle.

### Why is my Facebook cover photo cropped?

Computers and phones show different parts of the cover. Use a big image and keep important content in the middle.

### What size is a Facebook group cover photo?

A wide image around 1640 x 856 pixels is often recommended. Keep the important content in the middle.

### Why do my Facebook images look blurry?

Usually because the upload was much bigger or smaller than recommended, or already heavily compressed. Upload at the recommended size, and use PNG for graphics with text.

### Should I upload photos to Facebook as PNG or JPG?

JPG for photos, which keeps files small and looks natural. PNG for graphics with text, logos or sharp lines, because it keeps the edges crisp after Facebook processes the image.
