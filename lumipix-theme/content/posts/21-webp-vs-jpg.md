You save an image from a website and it arrives as a `.webp` file that some apps refuse to open. WebP has become one of the most common image formats on the web. Here is what it is and what to do with it.

## What is WebP?

WebP is an image format developed by Google. It can compress images in two ways:

- **lossy**, like JPG, for photos
- **lossless**, like PNG, for graphics

It also supports **transparency** and **animation**. In other words, one format can replace JPG, PNG and GIF.

## WebP vs JPG

| | WebP | JPG |
|---|---|---|
| File size for photos | Usually smaller at similar quality | Larger |
| Transparency | Yes | No |
| Animation | Yes | No |
| Browser support | All modern browsers | Universal |
| Support in older software and forms | Patchy | Universal |

Google's own studies found lossy WebP files to be roughly a quarter to a third smaller than comparable JPGs. That is why websites use it: smaller images mean faster pages.

## Why can't I open my WebP file?

Most modern browsers and operating systems handle WebP. Problems usually come from:

- older photo editors and office software
- some online forms and portals that accept only JPG or PNG
- printing services

In those cases, convert it to JPG (for photos) or PNG (for graphics with transparency).

## Should you use WebP on your own website?

Yes, for most sites. WebP reduces page weight and improves loading speed, which helps both visitors and search rankings. Most website platforms and image plugins can serve WebP automatically. The Lumi Pix compressor can also save in WebP: set **Save as** to WebP.

For uploads to forms and services you do not control, stick with JPG. Our [PNG vs JPG guide](post:png-vs-jpg) covers the classic formats.

## What about AVIF?

AVIF is a newer format that often compresses even better than WebP, and modern browsers support it. It is a good option for websites, but support outside browsers is still catching up, so convert AVIF to JPG when an app or form will not accept it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is WebP better than JPG?

For websites, usually yes: smaller files at similar quality, plus transparency. For maximum compatibility with apps and forms, JPG is safer.

### How do I convert WebP to JPG for free?

Drop the file into a browser-based converter such as the Lumi Pix Image Resizer, choose JPG under Save as, and download.

### Does converting WebP to JPG reduce quality?

Slightly, because both are usually lossy. Use a high JPG quality and the difference is not visible.
