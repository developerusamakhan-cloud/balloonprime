You save an image from a website and it turns up as a `.webp` file that half your apps won't open. WebP has become one of the most common image formats on the web, so it's worth knowing what it is. Here's WebP vs JPG explained, and what to do when a WebP file gets in your way.

## What is WebP?

WebP is an image format made by Google. It can compress images in two ways:

- **lossy**, like JPG, for photos
- **lossless**, like PNG, for graphics

It also supports **transparency** and **animation**. So one format can do the job of JPG, PNG and GIF.

## WebP vs JPG

| | WebP | JPG |
|---|---|---|
| File size for photos | Usually smaller at similar quality | Bigger |
| Transparency | Yes | No |
| Animation | Yes | No |
| Browser support | All modern browsers | Everywhere |
| Support in older software and forms | Patchy | Everywhere |

Google's own studies found lossy WebP files to be roughly a quarter to a third smaller than comparable JPGs. That's why websites use it: smaller images, faster pages.

## Why won't my WebP file open?

Most modern browsers and operating systems handle WebP fine. Problems usually come from:

- older photo editors and office software
- online forms and portals that only accept JPG or PNG
- printing services

In those cases, convert it: to JPG for photos, or PNG for graphics with transparency.

## Where WebP files come from

- **Saving images from websites.** Lots of sites send WebP to browsers that support it, so "Save image as" gives you a .webp file.
- **Screenshots and downloads from some apps** that use WebP for smaller files.
- **WhatsApp and Telegram stickers**, which are often WebP.
- **Website builders and image plugins** that convert uploads to WebP automatically.

In every case the image itself is fine. It's just in a different wrapper from the JPG you were expecting.

## Keeping transparency: WebP to PNG

WebP images can have transparent backgrounds, like logos and stickers. Converting those to JPG fills the transparency with a colour. To keep it, [convert WebP to PNG](tool:webp-to-png) instead. PNG keeps every transparent pixel.

## Should you use WebP on your own website?

For most sites, yes. WebP makes pages lighter and faster, which helps your visitors and your search rankings. Most website platforms and image plugins can serve WebP automatically, or you can [convert PNG to WebP](tool:png-to-webp) yourself. The Lumi Pix compressor can save WebP too: just set **Save as** to WebP. If your browser can't make WebP, it falls back to JPG.

For uploads to forms and services you don't control, stick with JPG. Our [PNG vs JPG guide](post:png-vs-jpg) covers the classic formats.

## Should you save your own photos as WebP?

For photos you keep or send to friends, JPG is still the most compatible choice. WebP shines on websites, where every kilobyte affects loading speed. A sensible approach:

- **Keep your originals as JPG**, or HEIC on an iPhone.
- **Publish them on your website as WebP.**
- **Upload to forms as JPG**, unless the form says otherwise.

## WebP quality settings

Like JPG, lossy WebP has a quality setting. Around 75 to 85 usually looks the same as a JPG at a higher setting while being smaller.

## What about AVIF?

AVIF is a newer format that often compresses even better than WebP, and modern browsers support it. It's a good choice for websites, but support outside browsers is still catching up. When an app or form won't take an AVIF file, [convert AVIF to JPG](tool:avif-to-jpg).

## Speed and SEO

Search engines look at page speed, and images are usually the heaviest part of a page. Switching big images from JPG to WebP, and resizing them to the width they're shown at, can make pages noticeably faster, especially on mobile. For practical steps, see [how to lower image file size](post:how-to-lower-image-file-size).

## The short version

- **WebP is a modern format** from Google with lossy and lossless compression, transparency and animation.
- **It's usually smaller than JPG** at similar quality, which is why websites use it.
- **Convert to JPG** when an app, printer or form won't accept WebP.
- **Convert to PNG** instead when you need to keep a transparent background.
- **Use WebP on your own website**, and JPG for everyday sharing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is WebP better than JPG?

For websites, usually yes: smaller files at similar quality, plus transparency. For the best compatibility with apps and forms, JPG is safer.

### How do I convert WebP to JPG for free?

Drop the file into a browser converter like the Lumi Pix WebP to JPG tool and download the result.

### Does converting WebP to JPG reduce quality?

Slightly, because both are usually lossy. Use a high JPG quality and you won't see the difference.

### Why do images save as WebP from Google and other websites?

Websites send WebP because it loads faster. Your browser saves the file in whatever format the site sent.

### Can I convert WebP to PNG?

Yes. Use a converter and choose PNG, which also keeps any transparent background.

### Can WebP images be printed?

Many printing services and older photo apps don't accept WebP. Convert it to a high quality JPG first, then send it to print.
