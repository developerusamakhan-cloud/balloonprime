You save an image from a website and it arrives as a `.webp` file that some apps refuse to open. WebP has become one of the most common image formats on the web. Here is what it is and what to do with it.

## What is WebP?

WebP is an image format developed by Google. It can compress images in two ways:

- **lossy**, like JPG, for photos
- **lossless**, like PNG, for graphics

It also supports **transparency** and **animation**. In other words, one format can replace JPG, PNG and GIF.

## WebP vs JPG

| | WebP | JPG |
|---|---|---|
| File size for photos | Usually smaller at similar quality | Larger |
| Transparency | Yes | No |
| Animation | Yes | No |
| Browser support | All modern browsers | Universal |
| Support in older software and forms | Patchy | Universal |

Google's own studies found lossy WebP files to be roughly a quarter to a third smaller than comparable JPGs. That is why websites use it: smaller images mean faster pages.

## Why can't I open my WebP file?

Most modern browsers and operating systems handle WebP. Problems usually come from:

- older photo editors and office software
- some online forms and portals that accept only JPG or PNG
- printing services

In those cases, convert it to JPG (for photos) or PNG (for graphics with transparency).

## Should you use WebP on your own website?

Yes, for most sites. WebP reduces page weight and improves loading speed, which helps both visitors and search rankings. Most website platforms and image plugins can serve WebP automatically. The Lumi Pix compressor can also save in WebP: set **Save as** to WebP.

For uploads to forms and services you do not control, stick with JPG. Our [PNG vs JPG guide](post:png-vs-jpg) covers the classic formats.

## What about AVIF?

AVIF is a newer format that often compresses even better than WebP, and modern browsers support it. It is a good option for websites, but support outside browsers is still catching up, so convert AVIF to JPG when an app or form will not accept it.

## Where you will meet WebP files

- **Saving images from websites.** Many sites serve WebP to browsers that support it, so "Save image as" gives you a .webp file.
- **Screenshots and downloads from some apps** that default to WebP for smaller files.
- **WhatsApp and Telegram stickers**, which are often WebP.
- **Website builders and image plugins** that convert uploads to WebP automatically.

In all these cases the image itself is fine; it is just a different container from the JPG you might expect.

## Converting WebP to PNG to keep transparency

WebP images can have transparent backgrounds, for example logos and stickers. Converting those to JPG fills the transparency with a colour. To keep it:

1. Open the [Image Resizer](tool:image-resizer).
2. Set the unit to **%** at 100.
3. Choose **PNG** under **Save as**.
4. Download.

## Should you save your own photos as WebP?

For photos you keep or share with friends, JPG is still the most compatible choice. WebP shines on websites, where every kilobyte affects loading speed. A sensible approach:

- **Keep originals as JPG** (or HEIC on iPhone).
- **Publish on your website as WebP**, created automatically by your platform or manually with a converter.
- **Upload to forms as JPG**, unless the form says otherwise.

## WebP quality settings

Like JPG, lossy WebP has a quality setting. A quality of around 75–85 usually looks the same as a JPG at a higher setting while being smaller. The Lumi Pix [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image) can save WebP: choose **WebP** under **Save as** and set your target size. If your browser does not support creating WebP, it falls back to JPG automatically.

## Speed and SEO

Search engines consider page speed, and images are usually the heaviest part of a page. Switching large images from JPG to WebP and resizing them to the displayed width can make pages noticeably faster, especially on mobile connections. For practical steps, see [how to lower image file size](post:how-to-lower-image-file-size) and our [PNG vs JPG guide](post:png-vs-jpg). ## Summary

WebP is a modern, efficient format that does the jobs of JPG, PNG and GIF. Use it on websites, convert it to JPG when an app or form will not accept it, and keep JPG as your everyday format for sharing photos.

## Key takeaways

- **WebP is a modern format** from Google that supports lossy and lossless compression, transparency and animation.
- **It is usually smaller than JPG** at similar quality, which is why websites use it.
- **Convert to JPG** when an app, printer or online form will not accept WebP.
- **Convert to PNG** instead when you need to keep a transparent background.
- **Use WebP on your own website** for faster pages, and JPG for everyday sharing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is WebP better than JPG?

For websites, usually yes: smaller files at similar quality, plus transparency. For maximum compatibility with apps and forms, JPG is safer.

### How do I convert WebP to JPG for free?

Drop the file into a browser-based converter such as the Lumi Pix Image Resizer, choose JPG under Save as, and download.

### Does converting WebP to JPG reduce quality?

Slightly, because both are usually lossy. Use a high JPG quality and the difference is not visible.

### Why do images save as WebP from Google and other websites?

Websites serve WebP because it loads faster. Your browser saves the file in the format the site sent.

### Can I convert WebP to PNG?

Yes. Use a converter and choose PNG, which also keeps any transparent background.

### Can WebP images be printed?

Many printing services and older photo apps do not accept WebP. Convert the image to JPG first, at high quality, before sending it to print.
