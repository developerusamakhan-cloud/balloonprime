Few things are as frustrating as filling in a long online application, reaching the upload step and seeing "File size exceeds the maximum limit". Job portals, university admissions, scholarship forms, visa applications and public service commissions all set strict limits on photos and documents, and they rarely tell you how to meet them.

Here is a simple process that works for almost any form.

## Step 1: Find the exact requirements

Before touching the photo, read the instructions on the official advertisement or portal. Look for four things:

- **Maximum file size**, usually in KB (for example 50KB, 100KB or 200KB)
- **Dimensions**, in pixels or centimetres (for example 35×45 mm)
- **File format**, almost always JPG or JPEG
- **Background**, often plain white or light blue

Write these down. Requirements differ between organisations and can change between advertisements, so do not rely on what worked last year.

## Step 2: Prepare a good original

The cleaner the photo, the better it compresses.

- Stand in front of a plain, light wall in daylight.
- Keep the camera at eye level and look straight at it.
- Crop to head and shoulders unless the form says otherwise.

If the form asks for a specific size such as 35×45 mm, make the photo with the [passport size photo maker](tool:passport-size-photo) first. It crops to the right proportions at 300 DPI.

Open the [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image), type the limit from Step 1, and drop the photo. ## Step 4: Do the same for your signature and documents

Most forms also ask for a scanned signature and sometimes certificates or an ID card.

- **Signature:** sign with a dark pen on white paper, photograph it from directly above, crop tightly, then use the [signature resizer](tool:resize-signature). Limits between 10KB and 50KB are common.
- **Documents:** scan or photograph them flat, in good light, and use [compress to 200KB](tool:compress-image-to-200kb). Text stays readable at that size.

## Step 5: Check before you upload

Open the downloaded file and zoom in. Faces and text should be clear. Check the file name ends in `.jpg`. If the portal still rejects it, read the error carefully; it may be complaining about dimensions rather than file size.

## Common reasons forms still reject a photo

- **Wrong format:** iPhones save photos as HEIC. Lumi Pix saves as JPG automatically.
- **Wrong dimensions:** the file is small enough but the pixel size is wrong. Resize first.
- **Over the limit by a few bytes:** some portals count 1KB as 1,000 bytes. Lumi Pix aims under the stricter limit.
- **Browser issue:** try a different browser or clear the form and upload again.

## Frequently asked questions

### What photo size do most online forms accept?

There is no universal size. Limits between 20KB and 200KB are common, usually as JPG. Always check the official instructions for the form you are filling in.

### Can I compress my photo and signature together?

Yes. Drop both files into the compressor and set the limit. If the photo and signature have different limits, process them one at a time.

### Is it safe to compress ID documents online?

With Lumi Pix, yes: files are processed on your device and never uploaded. Avoid tools that upload sensitive documents to unknown servers.
