Websites, apps and forms often ask for an image at an exact size, such as 600 × 400 pixels, 1080 × 1080 or 1920 × 1080. Typing two numbers sounds easy, but if your photo is a different shape, the result can be stretched or badly cropped. Here is how to get it right.

## Step 1: Know your picture's current size

Every digital image is a grid of pixels. A typical phone photo is about 4000 × 3000. To see the size:

- **Windows:** right-click → Properties → Details → Dimensions
- **Mac:** open in Preview → Tools → Show Inspector
- **Lumi Pix:** drop the image into the [Image Resizer](tool:image-resizer) and the result list shows the size

## Step 2: Understand aspect ratio

Aspect ratio is the shape of the image: width divided by height. 4000 × 3000 is 4:3. 1920 × 1080 is 16:9. 1080 × 1080 is 1:1.

If the target has the **same ratio** as your photo, you can resize directly with no cropping. If the ratio is **different**, something has to give: either part of the image is cropped, or empty space is added, or the image is stretched.

## Step 3: Choose the right mode

**Keep proportions (lock on).** Type only the width and the height is calculated automatically. Use this when the exact height does not matter, for example "make it 1200 pixels wide".

**Fill (crop edges).** The image fills the exact size and the edges that do not fit are trimmed from the centre. Best for thumbnails and profile pictures.

**Fit (add background).** The whole image is kept and the remaining space is filled with a colour. Best when nothing may be cut off, such as a product or a document.

**Stretch.** Forces the exact size and distorts the image. Rarely what you want, except for abstract backgrounds.

## Step-by-step in the browser

1. Open the [Image Resizer](tool:image-resizer).
2. Drop one picture, or up to 20 to resize them all the same way.
3. To keep proportions, leave the **link** icon on and type the width. For an exact size, turn the link off and type both numbers.
4. Choose **Fit** or **Fill** and, for Fit, a background colour.
5. Pick a format under **Save as**, then download.

Your pictures are processed on your device and never uploaded.

## Making an image larger

Enlarging creates new pixels by blending existing ones. A modest increase looks fine, but doubling a small image makes it noticeably soft. If you need a large size, start from the largest original available.

## Resizing for a file size limit

Pixels and file size are related but not the same. If a form asks for an image under 100KB, reduce the pixels first and then use the [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image). Our guide on [reducing image size in KB](post:how-to-reduce-image-size-in-kb) explains the connection.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I resize an image to exact pixels without stretching?

Turn off the aspect lock, type the width and height, and choose Fill (to crop) or Fit (to add a background) instead of Stretch.

### How do I resize many pictures to the same size?

Drop up to 20 images into the resizer. The same width, height and fit mode are applied to all of them.

### What is the difference between pixels and DPI?

Pixels are the actual size of the image. DPI only tells a printer how many of those pixels to place in each inch of paper.
