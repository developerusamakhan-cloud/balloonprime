Nearly every online application asks for a scanned signature, usually as a small JPG with a strict size limit. The good news is that signatures are mostly white space, so they compress really well if you prepare them properly. Here's the whole process to resize a signature for an online form, for limits anywhere from 10KB to 50KB.

## What forms usually ask for

It varies, but signature uploads often specify:

- **Format:** JPG or JPEG
- **File size:** often between 10KB and 50KB
- **Dimensions:** sometimes a pixel size or a shape, like a wide rectangle
- **Background:** white, with dark ink

Check the official instructions for the exact numbers before you start.

## Step 1: Sign clearly

Use plain white paper and a black or dark blue pen. A gel or felt-tip pen gives a bolder line that survives compression much better than a faint ballpoint. Sign at a comfortable size, not tiny.

## Step 2: Take a good photo

Put the paper in good, even light, hold your phone straight above it, and avoid shadows. Get close enough that the signature fills most of the frame.

## Step 3: Crop it tight

Cut away the paper around the signature, leaving a small margin. The less empty space, the easier it is to hit a small limit and keep the line sharp. Most phone galleries have a crop tool.

## Step 4: Resize and compress

1. Open the tool to [resize signature online](tool:resize-signature).
2. Tap the limit from your form: 10KB, 20KB, 30KB or 50KB, or type your own.
3. Drop the cropped photo in.
4. Download the JPG. The **Fits target** badge tells you it's under the limit.

The tool also keeps the width modest, around 500 pixels, which is more than enough for a form and helps hit very small limits without blurring the ink.

If your form gives exact dimensions, set the width under **Advanced options**, or set the size in the [image resizer](tool:image-resizer) first.

## Making the paper whiter

Phone photos of paper often look grey or cream. Some portals accept that, but a clean background looks better and compresses smaller. You can:

- turn up brightness and contrast in your phone's photo editor before compressing, or
- [remove white background from image](tool:remove-white-background) files and then place the result on clean white.

Our guide on [removing the white background from a signature](post:remove-white-background-from-signature) covers the transparent version for documents.

## When things go wrong

**The form says the file is too large.** Pick a slightly smaller limit than the maximum. Some portals count 1KB as 1,000 bytes.

**The signature looks broken or faint.** The original line was too thin. Sign again with a bolder pen.

**The upload says invalid format.** Make sure the file ends in .jpg. iPhone photos may be HEIC, but Lumi Pix saves JPG automatically.

## The whole thing on a phone

Most people do their signature on a phone, so here's the full flow:

1. Sign on plain white paper with a dark pen.
2. Open the camera, hold the phone straight above the paper and take the photo in good light.
3. Open the photo in your gallery and use **Crop** to cut close around the signature.
4. If the paper looks grey, bump up **Brightness** and **Contrast** a little.
5. Open the signature resizer in your browser and tap the limit from the form.
6. Tap **Choose files**, pick the cropped photo and download the result.
7. Upload the downloaded file, not the original, in the form.

## When the form gives dimensions too

Some portals want exact pixel dimensions as well as a file size. Typical requests look like this:

| Requirement | What to do |
|---|---|
| A wide rectangle, like 300 x 80 pixels | Use the image resizer with the link off and Fit with white |
| A maximum width only | Set **Maximum width** under Advanced options in the signature tool |
| A size in cm, like 3.5 x 1.5 cm | Use the resizer with the unit set to **cm** at the stated DPI |
| Just a file size | Simply choose the KB limit |

When both dimensions and file size are given, resize first and compress second.

## Black ink or blue?

If the form says which colour, use that. Otherwise, black gives the strongest contrast and survives compression best. Blue is fine too, as long as it's dark. Avoid pencil, light blue and coloured gel pens, which can fade into the background after compression.

## Keep a set you can reuse

Once you've got a good signature, save it in a folder with your photo and document scans. Keep:

- the **original photo** of the signature
- a **JPG on white** for forms, compressed to a common limit like 20KB
- a **transparent PNG** for documents

Next time a form asks for a signature, just compress the original again to the new limit instead of signing all over again.

## The short version

- **Read the form's limits first:** file size, format and any dimensions.
- **Sign boldly** with a dark pen on plain white paper.
- **Crop tight** and brighten the paper before compressing.
- **Resize first, then compress** when both dimensions and KB limits are given.
- **Keep the original** so you can compress it again for the next form.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I reduce my signature to 20KB?

Crop the signature tight, open the signature resizer, pick 20KB and drop the photo in. Download the JPG once it says Fits target.

### What size should a signature image be?

Follow the form's instructions. A width of 300 to 600 pixels is usually plenty for clear online display.

### Can I use a transparent PNG signature on a form?

Only if the form accepts PNG. Most application portals want a JPG on a white background.

### Can I use a digital signature drawn on my phone?

If the form accepts it, yes. Many forms expect a scanned handwritten signature, so check the instructions first.

### Why is my signature rejected even though it is under the size limit?

The form may also check dimensions or format. Make sure it's a JPG and matches any stated pixel or cm size.

### Should I sign larger or smaller for a scanned signature?

Sign at your normal size, or slightly bigger if anything. A signature that's too small loses detail when it's compressed, while a bigger one can always be shrunk cleanly.
