Passport and ID photos follow strict rules, and the passport photo size is the first thing that gets checked. This guide explains the common sizes, converts them to pixels for printing and online uploads, and shows how to make one yourself.

## The two most common sizes

| Standard | Size | Pixels at 300 DPI |
|---|---|---|
| ISO / many countries | 35 × 45 mm | 413 × 531 px |
| United States and some others | 2 × 2 in (51 × 51 mm) | 600 × 600 px |

Many countries, including the UK, EU member states and much of Asia, use 35×45 mm. The United States uses a square 2×2 inch photo. Some countries and visa offices use other sizes, so **always confirm with the authority you are applying to**.

## How the pixel numbers are calculated

Print size and pixels are linked by DPI (dots per inch). There are 25.4 mm in an inch, so:

- 35 mm ÷ 25.4 × 300 = **413 pixels** wide
- 45 mm ÷ 25.4 × 300 = **531 pixels** tall

300 DPI is the standard for photo printing. A photo with fewer pixels than this will print soft at passport size.

## What else the rules usually require

Size is only part of it. Most authorities also specify:

- a **plain, light background**, often white or off-white
- the head centred and facing the camera, with a **neutral expression** and both eyes open
- even lighting with **no shadows** on the face or background
- no glasses glare, and head coverings only for religious or medical reasons
- a recent photo, typically taken within the last six months

Some specify the size of the head within the frame. For example, US passport guidance asks for the head to measure between 1 and 1 3/8 inches from chin to top of head. Check your authority's exact wording.

## How to make a passport photo at home

1. Stand about a metre in front of a plain light wall, facing a window for soft light.
2. Ask someone to take the photo at eye level, from a little distance, so your face is not distorted.
3. Open the [passport photo maker](tool:passport-size-photo) and drop the photo.
4. Choose **Passport 35×45 mm** or **US passport 2×2 in**. The tool crops to the right proportions at 300 DPI.
5. Download the JPG. The DPI is written into the file, so it prints at the correct physical size.

If the background is not plain, use the [Background Remover](tool:background-remover) first, choose white as the new background, then resize.

## Uploading to an online application

Online forms often add a **file size limit** on top of the dimensions. After making the photo, use the [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image) and type the limit from the form, or follow our guide to [compressing photos for online application forms](post:compress-photo-for-online-forms).

## Printing

Most photo labs print on 4×6 inch (10×15 cm) paper. You can place several passport photos on one sheet with a photo editor, or print a single photo and trim it carefully along the edges.

## Getting the head size right

Many authorities specify how large the head should be inside the frame, measured from chin to the top of the head. A practical way to get close:

1. Take the photo from about 1–1.5 metres away, not close up, so the face is not distorted.
2. Leave space above the head and around the shoulders.
3. When the tool crops to the passport shape, the face should fill roughly two thirds to three quarters of the height for most standards.

If the crop cuts too close to your hair, start from a photo taken from slightly further away so there is more room around you.

## Background colour by requirement

Most standards ask for plain white or light grey, but some ask for other colours, such as light blue for certain visa applications. To change the background:

1. Open the [Background Remover](tool:background-remover) and drop the photo.
2. Pick **white**, or choose a custom colour that matches the requirement.
3. Download as JPG, then open it in the passport photo maker to crop to size.

Always use a soft, even colour. Gradients, shadows and textures can lead to rejection.

## Digital submissions vs printed photos

| | Printed photo | Digital upload |
|---|---|---|
| What matters | Physical size and print quality | Pixel size, file size, format |
| DPI | 300 for a sharp print | Usually not checked |
| File size | Not relevant | Often limited, e.g. 50–240KB |
| Format | Glossy or matte photo paper | Usually JPG |

For online applications, check both the pixel dimensions and the KB limit. Make the photo first, then compress it with the [Image Compressor](tool:compress-image) if needed. The compressor keeps the 300 DPI value, so the same file still prints correctly.

## Printing several photos on one sheet

Many forms ask for two to six identical photos. Instead of printing one photo per sheet, place several 35×45 mm photos on a single 4×6 inch (10×15 cm) canvas in a photo editor, leaving a thin gap to cut along. At 300 DPI, a 4×6 sheet is 1200×1800 pixels, which fits up to six 35×45 mm photos (two across, three down) with room to cut.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size is a passport photo in pixels?

At 300 DPI, a 35×45 mm photo is 413×531 pixels and a 2×2 inch photo is 600×600 pixels.

### Can I take my own passport photo?

In many countries, yes, as long as it meets the official rules for size, background, lighting and expression. Check the issuing authority's guidance.

### Does the tool check face position and expression?

No. It handles size and resolution. You are responsible for making sure the photo meets the biometric rules of your authority.

### What size is a passport photo in the UK and EU?

Most use 35×45 mm. That is 413×531 pixels at 300 DPI.

### Can I wear glasses in a passport photo?

Many authorities now ask you to remove glasses to avoid glare. Check the current rules of the office you are applying to.
