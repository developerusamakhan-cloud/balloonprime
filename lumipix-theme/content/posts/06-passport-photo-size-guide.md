Passport and ID photos have strict rules, and the passport photo size is the first thing anyone checks. This guide goes through the common sizes, turns them into pixels for printing and online uploads, and shows you how to make one yourself at home.

## The two sizes you'll see most

| Standard | Size | Pixels at 300 DPI |
|---|---|---|
| ISO, used by many countries | 35 x 45 mm | 413 x 531 px |
| United States and a few others | 2 x 2 in (51 x 51 mm) | 600 x 600 px |

Lots of countries, including the UK, the EU and much of Asia, use 35 x 45 mm. The United States uses a square 2 x 2 inch photo. Some countries and visa offices use other sizes, so **always check with the office you're applying to**.

## Where the pixel numbers come from

Print size and pixels are linked by DPI (dots per inch). There are 25.4 mm in an inch, so:

- 35 mm divided by 25.4, times 300 = **413 pixels** wide
- 45 mm divided by 25.4, times 300 = **531 pixels** tall

300 DPI is the standard for photo printing. A photo with fewer pixels than that will print soft at passport size.

## What else the rules usually ask for

Size is only part of it. Most authorities also want:

- a **plain, light background**, often white or off-white
- your head centred and facing the camera, with a **neutral expression** and both eyes open
- even light with **no shadows** on your face or the background
- no glare from glasses, and head coverings only for religious or medical reasons
- a recent photo, usually taken in the last six months

Some also set the size of your head in the frame. US passport guidance, for example, asks for the head to measure between 1 and 1 3/8 inches from chin to the top of the head. Check the exact wording for your application.

## Making a passport photo at home

1. Stand about a metre in front of a plain, light wall, facing a window for soft light.
2. Ask someone to take the photo at eye level from a little way back, so your face isn't distorted.
3. Open the [passport size photo creator](tool:passport-size-photo) and drop the photo in.
4. Choose **Passport 35 x 45 mm** or **US passport 2 x 2 in**. The tool crops to the right shape at 300 DPI.
5. Download the JPG. The DPI is saved in the file, so it prints at the right size.

If the background isn't plain, make a [passport photo with white background](tool:white-background-photo) first, then crop it to size. Our guide on [how to take a passport photo at home](post:take-passport-photo-at-home) goes into lighting and setup in more detail.

## Getting the head size about right

Many authorities say how big your head should be in the frame, measured from chin to the top of the head. A practical way to get close:

1. Take the photo from about 1 to 1.5 metres away, not up close.
2. Leave space above your head and around your shoulders.
3. When the tool crops to the passport shape, your face should fill roughly two thirds to three quarters of the height for most standards.

If the crop cuts too close to your hair, start with a photo taken from a bit further back.

## Background colours

Most standards want plain white or light grey, but a few ask for something else, like light blue for some visa applications. To change it:

1. Open the [remove background for free online](tool:background-remover) tool and drop the photo in.
2. Pick **white**, or a custom colour that matches the rules.
3. Download as JPG, then crop it to size in the passport photo creator.

Stick to a soft, even colour. Gradients, shadows and textures can get a photo rejected.

## Printed photo or digital upload?

| | Printed photo | Digital upload |
|---|---|---|
| What matters | Physical size and print quality | Pixel size, file size, format |
| DPI | 300 for a sharp print | Usually not checked |
| File size | Doesn't matter | Often limited, say 50 to 240KB |
| Format | Glossy or matte photo paper | Usually JPG |

For online applications, check both the pixel dimensions and the KB limit. Make the photo first, then use the [image compressor](tool:compress-image) if you need to. It keeps the 300 DPI value, so the same file still prints correctly. Our guide on [how to compress a photo for online application forms](post:compress-photo-for-online-forms) covers the upload side.

## Printing several copies on one sheet

Lots of forms want two to six identical photos. You don't need to print one per sheet. In the passport photo creator, choose **10 x 15 cm** or **4 x 6 in photo paper** under Print sheet and it lays out as many copies as fit, with thin cut lines. A 35 x 45 mm photo gives you six copies on one sheet, and a 2 x 2 inch photo gives you two. Print it at 100% scale, then cut along the lines.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size is a passport photo in pixels?

At 300 DPI, a 35 x 45 mm photo is 413 x 531 pixels and a 2 x 2 inch photo is 600 x 600 pixels.

### Can I take my own passport photo?

In many countries, yes, as long as it meets the official rules for size, background, lighting and expression. Check the issuing authority's guidance.

### Does the tool check face position and expression?

No. It handles size and resolution. Making sure the photo meets the biometric rules of your authority is up to you.

### What size is a passport photo in the UK and EU?

Most use 35 x 45 mm. That's 413 x 531 pixels at 300 DPI.

### Can I wear glasses in a passport photo?

Many authorities now ask you to take glasses off to avoid glare. Check the current rules of the office you're applying to.
