A logo with a white box around it looks a bit amateur on coloured websites, slides and T-shirts. What you want is a transparent background. If your logo sits on a flat colour, you can make the logo background transparent in a few seconds.

## The quick way

1. Open the tool to [remove white background from image](tool:remove-white-background) files.
2. Drop your logo in. The white areas turn transparent straight away.
3. If the background isn't white, click **Pick from image** and then click the background.
4. Adjust **Tolerance** until the background disappears completely without eating into the logo.
5. Adjust **Edge softness** for smooth edges.
6. Download as **PNG**.

The preview shows the result on a checkerboard, which is how transparency is usually shown.

## Getting clean edges

Logo edges are usually smoothed, which means the pixels along the edge are a mix of the logo colour and the background. Simple tools either leave a pale fringe or chop the edges harshly. The Lumi Pix tool fades edge pixels smoothly and **takes the background colour out of them**, so the logo looks right on dark backgrounds too.

If you see a faint halo:

- raise **Edge softness** a little
- check you picked the actual background colour, not a shade close to it

## When the logo uses the background colour

If your logo has white parts inside it, like white text on a red circle, those would turn transparent too. In that case:

- use a lower tolerance so only the pure background goes, and check the inside areas, or
- [remove background for free online](tool:background-remover) with the AI tool, which finds the object rather than a colour, or
- go back to the original design file and export it with transparency, which is always the cleanest option.

## Which format?

- **PNG** keeps transparency and works everywhere.
- **WebP** supports transparency too, with smaller files, so it's great for websites.
- **JPG doesn't support transparency.** Save as JPG and the background fills with a colour again.

Our [PNG vs JPG guide](post:png-vs-jpg) goes into the formats in more depth.

## A logo photographed on a sign or shirt

If the logo was photographed on a sign, a T-shirt or a textured surface, removing by colour will struggle. Use the AI tool, or ask the designer for the original file. For more methods, see [how to remove the background from an image](post:how-to-remove-background-from-image).

## Is it really transparent?

A checkerboard preview means transparency, but some image viewers show transparent areas as white or black, which is confusing. To be sure:

- Open the PNG in a browser tab. Transparent areas show the page colour.
- Put it on a coloured slide in PowerPoint or Google Slides.
- Look at it in dark mode. Any leftover white box jumps out straight away.

## Logos on dark backgrounds

A dark logo on a dark website disappears even with a transparent background. Common fixes:

- Make a **light version** of the logo for dark backgrounds.
- Add a subtle **white outline** or shadow in a design tool.
- Put the logo on a light **badge or circle** that sits on the dark background.

If your site has a dark mode, get both versions ready now so you're not caught out later.

## Vector logos are even better

If your logo was designed in Illustrator, Figma or something similar, ask for an **SVG** or **PDF** version. Vector files are sharp at any size and already transparent. When an app doesn't take vectors, use the [online SVG to PNG converter](tool:svg-to-png) to export a PNG at a generous size so it stays crisp.

## Cleaning up a logo that's only on paper

Logos that only exist on paper, like old letterheads or stamps, can be brought into the digital world:

1. Scan or photograph the logo flat in good light.
2. Crop tight, then brighten it and add contrast so the paper looks almost white.
3. Remove the white background to make the paper transparent.
4. If the edges look rough, raise **Edge softness**.

For a perfectly clean result, a designer can trace the scan into a vector logo.

## A logo on a coloured box

If the logo sits on a solid coloured box, say white text on a blue rectangle, decide which part you want to keep. To remove the blue, click **Pick from image** and then the blue. To keep the box and only remove the white around it, pick white. It works the same way for stamps and badges. The same tool also makes a [transparent signature](post:remove-white-background-from-signature) for documents.

## The short version

- **Flat backgrounds** come off instantly with a colour tool. Busy ones need AI.
- **Pick the exact background colour** if it isn't pure white.
- **Use edge softness** to avoid jagged edges and pale halos.
- **Save as PNG or WebP** to keep transparency. JPG fills it in again.
- **Make a light version** of your logo for dark backgrounds and dark mode.
- **Ask for a vector file** (SVG or PDF) whenever you can.
- **Test it** on white, black and coloured backgrounds before using it everywhere.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I remove the white background from a logo?

Drop the logo into a white background remover, adjust the tolerance if needed, and download it as a PNG.

### Why does my transparent logo have a white outline?

The edge pixels still have some white in them. Raise the edge softness, or use a tool that removes the background colour from the edges.

### Can I make a JPG logo transparent?

Yes. Remove the background and save the result as PNG, because JPG can't store transparency.

### What size should a transparent logo be?

Export it at about twice the size it's shown, like 120 pixels tall for a 60 pixel header, so it stays sharp on high resolution screens.

### Is SVG better than PNG for logos?

For websites and printing, SVG is sharper at any size. Use PNG when an app or platform doesn't accept SVG.

### Can I make a logo transparent on my phone?

Yes. Open the white background remover in your phone's browser, pick the logo from your gallery and download the PNG. It works exactly like it does on a computer.
