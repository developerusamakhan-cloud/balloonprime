Application portals often ask for copies of ID cards, degrees, transcripts and certificates, usually as JPG files under 200KB or so. Text is less forgiving than photos: compress it too hard and letters smear together. Here is how to keep documents crisp.

## Step 1: Capture the document properly

- Lay the document **flat** on a dark, plain surface so the edges stand out.
- Use **daylight** or even lighting, and avoid the shadow of your phone.
- Hold the phone **directly above** and parallel to the page.
- Many phones have a document scanner built into the camera or notes app, which straightens the page automatically. Use it if you have it.

## Step 2: Crop to the page

Remove the table or floor around the document. Cropping reduces the pixel count and keeps the file budget for the text itself.

## Step 3: Compress to the limit

1. Open [compress image to 200KB](tool:compress-image-to-200kb).
2. Drop the scan (or several pages at once).
3. Download once each file shows **Fits target**.

200KB leaves enough room for a full A4 page to remain readable at around 1500–2000 pixels wide. The tool reduces dimensions in small steps only when needed, so small print stays legible. Because the work happens on your device, your ID and certificates are never uploaded.

## Check readability

Open the result and zoom to 100%. Can you read the smallest text, such as dates and registration numbers? If not:

- re-scan with better lighting,
- crop more tightly, or
- if the form allows, use a slightly higher limit.

## Multi-page documents

If a form wants one file for several pages, it usually asks for PDF. If it accepts separate images, compress each page individually. Some portals limit the total size, so divide the limit between the pages.

## Colour or black and white?

Unless the form asks for colour, a clean greyscale scan often compresses smaller and reads more clearly. Your phone's photo editor can convert to black and white before you compress.

## Scanning with your phone's built-in scanner

Most phones can scan documents without an extra app:

- **iPhone:** open the **Notes** app, create a note, tap the camera icon and choose **Scan Documents**. The page is detected, straightened and cleaned up automatically. Save it, then share it as an image.
- **Android:** the **Google Drive** app has a **Scan** option, and many camera apps have a document mode that crops and straightens pages.

These modes produce cleaner, flatter scans than a normal photo, which then compress better.

## The best settings for different documents

| Document | Colour | Suggested target | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| CNIC or ID card (one side) | Colour | 100–200KB | Crop tightly to the card |
| Degree or certificate | Colour or greyscale | 200KB | Keep seals and signatures readable |
| Transcript with small text | Greyscale | 200–300KB | Small text needs more pixels |
| Typed letter | Greyscale | 100–200KB | Text compresses well |
| Handwritten notes | Greyscale | 200KB | Increase contrast before compressing |

If the form allows more than 200KB for a dense transcript, use the higher limit.

## Making text sharper before compressing

1. **Increase contrast** slightly in your phone's photo editor so text is darker and paper is whiter.
2. **Straighten the page** so lines of text are horizontal.
3. **Crop away the background** completely.
4. **Avoid zooming in heavily** when photographing; move the phone closer instead.

These small steps make a bigger difference to readability than any compression setting.

## ID cards: front and back

Many forms ask for both sides of an ID card, sometimes in one image. To combine them:

1. Photograph each side separately and crop each one to the card.
2. Use the [Image Resizer](tool:image-resizer) to make both the same width.
3. Place them one above the other in any simple editor, or upload separately if the form allows.
4. Compress the final image to the limit.

If the form gives separate upload fields for front and back, keep them as separate files.

## Checking the scan before submitting

Open the compressed file and zoom to 100%. Read the smallest line, such as the date of issue or a registration number. If you cannot read it easily, neither can the person reviewing your application. Re-scan in better light, crop tighter, or use a higher limit if allowed.

For photos and signatures to go with your documents, see our guides on [compressing a photo to 100KB](post:compress-image-to-100kb-guide) and [resizing a signature](post:resize-signature-for-online-form). For general tips, read [how to reduce image size in KB](post:how-to-reduce-image-size-in-kb).

## Keeping digital copies organised

Create one folder for each application with clearly named files, such as `cnic-front.jpg`, `cnic-back.jpg`, `degree.jpg` and `photo.jpg`. Keep the originals in a separate subfolder. Next time you apply, you only need to re-compress to the new limits.

## Key takeaways

- **Use your phone's document scanner** for flat, straight, evenly lit scans.
- **Crop to the page** and increase contrast slightly before compressing.
- **200KB keeps a full page readable** at around 1500–2000 pixels wide.
- **Greyscale** often reads better and compresses smaller for typed text.
- **Zoom to 100%** and read the smallest text before you upload.
- **Name files clearly** and keep originals in a separate folder for future applications.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I reduce a scanned document to 200KB?

Crop the scan to the page, then use a 200KB target compressor. It finds the highest quality that keeps the file under 200KB.

### Will the text still be readable at 200KB?

For a single page, yes, as long as the scan is sharp and well lit. Check the result at 100% zoom.

### Is it safe to compress my ID card online?

Use a tool that processes files on your device, such as Lumi Pix, so your ID is never uploaded to a server.

### How do I scan a document with my phone?

On iPhone, use Notes → Scan Documents. On Android, use the Scan option in Google Drive or your camera's document mode.

### Should document scans be colour or black and white?

Use colour for ID cards and certificates with seals. Greyscale is often clearer and smaller for typed or handwritten text.

### Can I upload a photo of a document instead of a scan?

Usually yes, if the form accepts images. Photograph the page flat in good light, crop it to the edges, and compress it. A phone's document scanner mode gives the cleanest result.
