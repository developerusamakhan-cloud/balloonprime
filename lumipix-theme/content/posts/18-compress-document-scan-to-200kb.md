Application portals often want copies of your ID card, degrees, transcripts and certificates, usually as JPG files under about 200KB. Text is less forgiving than photos: squeeze it too hard and the letters smear together. Here's how to compress a document scan to 200KB and keep every word crisp.

## Step 1: Capture the document properly

- Lay it **flat** on a dark, plain surface so the edges stand out.
- Use **daylight** or even light, and watch out for your phone's shadow.
- Hold the phone **straight above** the page and parallel to it.
- Lots of phones have a document scanner built into the camera or notes app, which straightens the page for you. Use it if you've got it.

## Step 2: Crop to the page

Cut away the table or floor around the document. Fewer pixels means more of the file goes to the text itself.

## Step 3: Compress it to the limit

1. Open the page to [compress image to 200KB](tool:compress-image-to-200kb).
2. Drop the scan in, or several pages at once.
3. Download once each file says **Fits target**.

200KB leaves enough room for a full A4 page to stay readable at around 1500 to 2000 pixels wide. The tool only brings the dimensions down in small steps when it has to, so the small print stays legible. And because it all happens on your device, your ID and certificates are never uploaded.

## Can you read it?

Open the result and zoom to 100%. Can you read the smallest text, like dates and registration numbers? If not:

- scan it again in better light,
- crop tighter, or
- use a slightly higher limit if the form allows it.

## Using your phone's built-in scanner

Most phones can scan documents without an extra app:

- **iPhone.** Open **Notes**, make a new note, tap the camera icon and choose **Scan Documents**. It finds the page, straightens it and cleans it up. Save it, then share it as an image.
- **Android.** The **Google Drive** app has a **Scan** option, and many camera apps have a document mode that crops and straightens pages.

These give you cleaner, flatter scans than a normal photo, and those compress better.

## The best settings for each document

| Document | Colour | Suggested target | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| CNIC or ID card (one side) | Colour | 100 to 200KB | Crop tight to the card |
| Degree or certificate | Colour or greyscale | 200KB | Keep seals and signatures readable |

| Typed letter | Greyscale | 100 to 200KB | Text compresses well |
| Handwritten notes | Greyscale | 200KB | Add contrast before compressing |

If the form allows more than 200KB for a dense transcript, use the higher limit.

## Making text sharper before you compress

1. **Add a little contrast** in your phone's photo editor so the text is darker and the paper whiter.
2. **Straighten the page** so lines of text run level.
3. **Crop away the background** completely.
4. **Don't zoom in a lot** when photographing. Move the phone closer instead.

These small steps make more difference to readability than any compression setting.

## Colour or black and white?

Unless the form asks for colour, a clean greyscale scan often compresses smaller and reads more clearly. Your phone's photo editor can switch it to black and white before you compress.

## Several pages

If a form wants several pages in one file, it usually asks for a PDF. Compress each page first, then [make JPG to PDF online](tool:image-to-pdf) in the right order. If it takes separate images, compress each page on its own. Some portals limit the total size, so split that limit between the pages.

## ID cards: front and back

Many forms ask for both sides of an ID card. If there are separate upload fields for front and back, keep them as two files. If the form wants both sides in one file, photograph each side, crop each one to the card, and put them on two pages of a PDF, or place them one above the other in a simple editor. Then compress the final file to the limit.

## Check it before you submit

Open the compressed file, zoom to 100% and read the smallest line, like the issue date or a registration number. If you can't read it easily, neither can the person checking your application. Scan again in better light, crop tighter, or use a higher limit if it's allowed.

Make one folder per application with clearly named files, like `cnic-front.jpg`, `cnic-back.jpg`, `degree.jpg` and `photo.jpg`. Keep the originals in a separate folder. Next time you apply, you only need to compress them to the new limits.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I reduce a scanned document to 200KB?

Crop the scan to the page, then use a 200KB target compressor. It finds the highest quality that keeps the file under 200KB.

### Will the text still be readable at 200KB?

For a single page, yes, as long as the scan is sharp and well lit. Check it at 100% zoom.

### Is it safe to compress my ID card online?

Use a tool that processes files on your device, like Lumi Pix, so your ID is never uploaded to a server.

### How do I scan a document with my phone?

On iPhone, use Notes and Scan Documents. On Android, use the Scan option in Google Drive or your camera's document mode.

### Should document scans be colour or black and white?

Colour for ID cards and certificates with seals. Greyscale is often clearer and smaller for typed or handwritten text.

### Can I upload a photo of a document instead of a scan?

Usually yes, if the form accepts images. Photograph the page flat in good light, crop it to the edges and compress it. A phone's document scanner mode gives the cleanest result.
