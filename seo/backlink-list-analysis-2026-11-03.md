# Backlink list analysis: is it worth creating accounts everywhere?

**Date:** 3 November 2026
**Source list:** https://ghulamaliseo.com/backlinks/ (128 sites, ranked by Domain Rating)
**Question:** If Vyntic creates a profile on every site and puts all five product URLs in the description, will the products benefit?

**Products checked against the list**
| Product | What it is (from the live site) |
|---|---|
| Talkwyn (talkwyn.com) | AI chatbot for WordPress that answers visitors in their own language |
| Lumi Pix (lumipix.tools) | Free, private image tools that run in the browser |
| Testmatik (testmatik.app) | Free online hardware tests, Turkish language |
| Claim Fairly (claimfairly.com) | Free car accident claim calculators |
| Power Bachat (powerbachat.com) | Electricity bill and solar calculators for Pakistan, India and Bangladesh |
| Vyntic Studio (vyntic.studio) | The studio behind them (WordPress development and plugins) |

**About the data:** DR values and the "100% live" claim come from the source page and were not verified. The page promotes an SEO course. Whether a link is followed or nofollow was not tested for each site; it depends on the site and can change, so check it on the live listing (see "How to check" at the end). Where a site's purpose could not be confirmed, the table says so.

---

## Short answer

**No, not the way it is planned.** One profile with all five URLs copied to 128 sites will bring very little:

1. **Most profile and description links are nofollow.** They pass little or no ranking value.
2. **Most profile pages are never indexed.** A new profile has no links pointing to it, so Google often ignores it. A link on an unindexed page counts for nothing.
3. **No relevance.** A bundle of an AI chatbot, a Turkish hardware tester, car claim calculators and electricity calculators on one page tells Google nothing about any of them.
4. **Pattern risk.** The same description with the same five links across dozens of sites is the kind of pattern Google's link spam policy describes. The usual outcome is that the links are ignored, not a penalty, but it is wasted work.
5. **Many sites on the list cannot be used at all.** 28 of the 128 are editorial media, podcasts or image hosts where you cannot place a link yourself, or that have nothing to do with these products.

**What does work:** a separate, proper listing **for each product** on the directories that match it, with its own description, screenshots and category. That gives real referral visitors, helps Google discover and understand each product, and some of these listings are followed links. From this list that means roughly **30 to 40 listings for Talkwyn, around 10 for Lumi Pix, and only a handful for the other three.**

## What to do for the products this list does not cover
- **Testmatik:** Turkish tech forums and Turkish "useful websites" lists. A Turkish audience will not find it on English startup directories.
- **Claim Fairly:** legal and insurance resource pages, personal finance blogs (guest posts with a genuinely useful calculator), and local business citations in the target country.
- **Power Bachat:** solar installer blogs, energy and consumer forums, and local communities in Pakistan, India and Bangladesh. Calculators are naturally linkable; the best links come from people who embed or cite them.

## Recommended order
1. Build a **"Products" page on vyntic.studio** that links to all five products, and link each product's footer back to Vyntic. This is the one place where listing all five together is correct, and you control it.
2. **One Vyntic company profile** each on GitHub, Indie Hackers, Indie Page, Clutch and GoodFirms.
3. **Talkwyn:** G2, Capterra, AlternativeTo, SaaSHub, then the AI directories, then a Product Hunt launch.
4. **Lumi Pix:** AlternativeTo, SaaSHub, Alternative Me, then a Product Hunt launch on a different day.
5. Launch platforms (Fazier, Uneed, Microlaunch, TinyLaunch, LaunchIgniter, Peerlist) for Talkwyn first, then Lumi Pix.
6. Reddit and Quora answers for Power Bachat and Claim Fairly, only where the calculator actually answers the question.

## Rules for every listing
- **One product per listing**, with its own description. Do not paste the same text everywhere.
- Use the product's real facts only. Do not add ratings, user counts or reviews that do not exist.
- Pick the most specific category the site offers.
- Add real screenshots and a logo; listings without them are often rejected or ignored.
- Keep a simple sheet: site, product, date submitted, listing URL, followed or nofollow, indexed or not.

## How to check if a listing actually helps
1. Open the live listing, right-click your link and choose Inspect. If the link has `rel="nofollow"`, `ugc` or `sponsored`, it passes little ranking value.
2. Search Google for `site:` plus the listing URL after two to four weeks. If it does not appear, the page is not indexed and the link does not count yet.
3. Check referral visits in analytics. For directories, real visitors are the main benefit.
